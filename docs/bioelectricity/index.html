STEP 1

D'Arcy Thompson's 1917 "Rubber Graph-Paper" Idea

In 1917, Scottish biologist and mathematician D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson asked a simple question: When one fish species evolves into another, does nature have to redesign every single bone from scratch? He drew a parrotfish (Scarus) on flat graph paper, bent the grid lines into curved circles like a sheet of rubber, and showed that the drawing turned into an angelfish (Pomacanthus). He argued that an embryo only needs to turn a few global "growth knobs" to transform one body plan into another.

STEP 2

Mistake #1: Why Computers Thought Thompson Was Wrong ("The 17-Dot Head Trap")

For the last 30 years, biologists tested Thompson's idea on computers and concluded it failed across fish families. Why? Because instead of tracing the whole outline of the fish, standard fish databases only measure 17 dots on the skeleton: 9 of those 17 dots (53% of the math weight) are crammed inside the head and jaws, and zero dots (0%) are on the actual outer fins or tail!

Why the 17 Dots Failed: Imagine trying to tailor a full-body wetsuit by measuring 9 points on someone's face and 0 points on their arms or legs! Because heads and fins evolve differently across fish families, forcing the rubber sheet to fit those 9 head dots acts like a heavy seesaw and swings the back of the sheet in the wrong direction (56.2% mismatch — worse than not stretching the fish at all!).
STEP 3

Mistake #2: Why "Just Stretching a Box" Fails True Anatomy — And Why Thompson's Curves Win

When we switch from 17 skeleton dots to a 600-point outline of the whole fish, how do we know Thompson's slanted and curved grids are really needed, instead of just stretching a plain rectangle taller or wider ("Plain Box Stretch")?

The "Shadow Cheat" vs. True Anatomy: A plain rectangular Box Stretch lifts the middle of the back straight up (red X in Panel A), missing the swept-back fin tips of the angelfish (only 2.4% match!). Once you require the grid to map the true Dorsal Fin Tip, Anal Fin Tip, and fin bases along the body (without the 9 internal skull dots), a plain Box Stretch fails and Thompson's curved and slanted grids jump by +13% to +48% accuracy!

Testing three independent datasets totaling 10,303 fish species across 987 genera and 440 families revealed that fish evolution obeys two simple rules working at the same time:
