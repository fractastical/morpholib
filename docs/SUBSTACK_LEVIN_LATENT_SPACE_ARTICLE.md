## Gallery I · Exhibit 1 — Claim 1: "No Error-Handling Code Was Added" to Self-Sorting Cells ([Zhang, Goldstein & Levin 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/10597123241269740))

*Wall label · Gallery I, The Self-Sorting Cells · Source: Zhang, Goldstein & Levin (2025),* Adaptive Behavior*; Levin (2026),* Ingressing Minds *§6.3, Fig. 14 · Medium: bit-exact ports of [the authors’ pinned GitHub code](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research/tree/1fd2bd5921c1f6b423a71f691d5189106a8a1020) in Python 3, C++20, Node.js, and Lean 4 (Theorem 2) · Plates III–VI.*

In [*Adaptive Behavior* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/10597123241269740) and Section 6.3 / Figure 14 of [*Ingressing Minds* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3390/philosophies11050161), Taining Zhang, Adam Goldstein, and Michael Levin asked a clever question: **What if there is no central controller (no "gym teacher") sorting an array of numbers from 1 to 100?** What if each number is an independent thread (an autonomous "cell") that can only look at its neighbors and decide on its own whether to swap places?

They gave the cells three classical computer-science "personalities" (**algotypes**):
* **Bubble cells (B):** Look at your immediate left or right neighbor (`p − 1` or `p + 1`). If you are out of order, swap!
* **Insertion cells (I):** Look at everyone to your left (`0 .. p − 1`). Only step left with `p − 1` if everyone to your left is already in order.
* **Selection cells (S):** Keep a mental finger (`ideal_position` / `target` pointer) starting at slot `0`, then `1`, then `2`... If your number is smaller than whoever is standing at `target`, jump straight there and swap with them; otherwise move your finger one slot to the right.

Now look at the first philosophical claim Levin makes about this code in *Ingressing Minds* (Section 6.3, p. 22) when a few cells in the array are "frozen" (broken and refusing to move):

> *"The critical thing is that we did not add steps to the algorithm to check whether a swap had been completed, or to do specific things if an operation failed."*

**What we found in the code (Panel A):**
For **Bubble** sort, Levin’s statement is true. The only place [`BubbleSortCell.py`](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research/blob/1fd2bd5921c1f6b423a71f691d5189106a8a1020/modules/multithread/BubbleSortCell.py) looks at a `FREEZE` flag is to decline a swap with a frozen neighbor. The frozen cell is a brick wall, and the sorting rule is unchanged. **Selection** sort ([`SelectionSortCell.py`](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research/blob/1fd2bd5921c1f6b423a71f691d5189106a8a1020/modules/multithread/SelectionSortCell.py)) is the same: its pointer jumps straight across an immovable defect, and the one `FREEZE` branch in the upstream code only nudges that pointer past a frozen target so it does not get stuck. A skip, not a rewrite (rule `[U4]` in our port guide).

**Insertion sort is the counterexample.** Here the authors did not skip the frozen cell. They rewrote the algorithm’s own correctness test so it could see through one.

Remember Insertion sort’s rule: a cell at position `p` refuses to move left unless the entire prefix `0 .. p − 1` to its left is already sorted. If a single frozen cell wearing `#99` sits near the front of the line, then the left side is *never* sorted—which under pure Insertion sort would permanently paralyze every cell to the right of `#99`!

When we opened [`InsertionSortCell.py` (lines 68–82)](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research/blob/1fd2bd5921c1f6b423a71f691d5189106a8a1020/modules/multithread/InsertionSortCell.py#L68-L82), we found **3 lines of explicit broken-cell exception handling**:

```python
for i in range(self.current_position[0]):
    if self.cells[i].status == CellStatus.FREEZE:
        prev = -1   # <-- Explicit exception handler resetting prefix check at frozen cells!
        continue
    if self.cells[i].value < prev:
        return False
    prev = self.cells[i].value
```

When we deleted those 3 lines of code (`without_explicit_freeze_reset` in **Panel A** below, red bars, and formally proved in Lean 4 Theorem 2), Insertion sort’s final error (`n = 60`, `f = 3` frozen cells) **jumped 4.5× higher (from 4.90 to 21.95)** as all cells behind the defect froze!

## Gallery I · Exhibit 2 — Claim 2: "Delayed Gratification" — Do Sorting Cells Really Go Against Their Gradient to Navigate Around a Barrier?

*Wall label · Gallery I, The Self-Sorting Cells · Source:* Ingressing Minds *§6.3, Fig. 14A–B; Zhang et al. (2025) `monotonicity_error` and Delayed Gratification metrics · Medium: pairwise inversion count* `I(v)` *on the symmetric group* `S_n`*, Lean 4 Theorem 1 (`bubble_adjacent_swap_preserves_left_partition`) · Plate VII.*

In *Ingressing Minds* (Section 6.3 and Figure 14A–B), Levin compares the sorting array to an agent walking around a wall. Two magnets separated by a piece of wood get stuck forever, because they cannot move against their gradient even for a moment. The paper’s error score ticks up before it comes down, and from that he argues the algorithm is *"temporarily going against its gradient to recoup greater gains later."*

Two things are wrong with that reading. Both are proved in Lean 4.

**No cell walks around the wall.** Bubble and Insertion cells only swap with the neighbor beside them. Freeze a cell at spot 20 and nothing to its left ever reaches its right; the two halves sort in isolation (Theorem 1, `bubble_adjacent_swap_preserves_left_partition`). Only Selection sort gets past, and only because its pointer is allowed to jump anywhere in the array.

**The rising score is the wrong ruler.** There are two ways to measure how scrambled a line is.

* **Ruler 1, `I(v)`, counts every pair** that is out of order anywhere in the line. This is the real energy. Every Bubble swap in the experiment lowers it by exactly 1: zero uphill steps out of 192 (the straight line in Panel B).
* **Ruler 2, `E(v)`, counts only neighbors.** It is the ruler the paper uses, and it can rise while the line gets closer to sorted. That is the jagged curve, 47 bumps.

Four cells are enough to see it. Start with `[2, 4, 1, 3]`. Ruler 1 sees three out-of-order pairs, `(2,1)`, `(4,1)`, and `(4,3)`. Ruler 2 sees one neighbor drop, `4 → 1`. Swap `4` with `1` and the line is `[2, 1, 4, 3]`. Ruler 1 falls from 3 to 2. Ruler 2 rises from 1 to 2, because one tall cliff broke into two small steps. No frozen cells are required.

For the last 30 years, evolutionary biologists tried to test Thompson’s idea on computer landmark databases—and concluded he was mostly wrong across fish families. **Why did their tests fail?**

When we audited the morphometrics literature across **three independent datasets scaling up to 10,303 fish species (`440` families)**, we uncovered two methodological traps that had hidden Thompson’s law for a century:

1. **Trap #1 — The "17-Dot Head Seesaw":**
   Standard fish databases ([Claverie & Wainwright 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.12502); [Friedman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syz029)) don't trace the whole outline of the fish—they measure **17 dots on the skeleton**, with **9 of those 17 dots (53%) packed inside the head and jaws** and **0% on the outer dorsal, anal, or tail fins**. Because jaws evolve rapidly for diet while the body evolves for swimming, fitting a global rubber sheet to those 17 dots lets the 9 head dots pull the back of the sheet the wrong way—making the warped fish silhouette look **worse than no warp at all** (`40.6% → 56.2%` mismatch on *Scarus* → *Pomacanthus*)!

2. **Trap #2 — The "Plain Box Stretch" Illusion vs. True Thompson Curves:**
   When we switch to a **600-point whole-body outline**, if you only ask a computer to overlap two *unlabeled black shadows*, a trivial **Rectangular Box Stretch** (stretching height vs. width with *zero* slant or curve) can "cheat" by stretching the middle of a pufferfish’s belly straight up to cover the back fins of an ocean sunfish!
   **Where Thompson’s curved and slanted grids (`70° Oblique Shear`, `Coaxial Circles`, `Bilinear Fan`, `Radial Flare`) are genuinely required** is when you force the grid to match **true homologous perimeter anatomy** (dorsal fin base & tip to dorsal fin base & tip, anal fin to anal fin, without the 9 internal skull dots): there, a plain Box Stretch fails (**`2.4%`** match on the swept-back fin tips of the angelfish), and **Thompson’s curved grids jump by `+13%` to `+48%` (`15×` better)** while staying **100% smooth and un-folded on `6 out of 6` pairs** (whereas standard 600-point Thin-Plate Splines ([Bookstein 1989](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.24792)) fold and crumple the interior tissue grid on `6 out of 6` pairs!).

## The Annex — So Have We Abandoned Dualism? Yes. But Not the Way You Think.

*Wall label · The Annex · Interlocutors: Michael Levin (on AI-generated critique), Joscha Bach, and the phenomenological-state-space research community · Exhibits: the auditor’s own blind spot, the "within / without" trap, the cargo cult, the phenomenological state space, the Deep Platonic AI Stack (Platonic Representation Hypothesis vs. Artificial Hivemind) · Why the `++` in the title is a loop, not a number.*

One of the points Levin makes as he responds to AI-generated prose—including the [36,000 words Nathan Sweet originally sent him](https://thoughtforms.life/qa-from-the-internet-and-recent-presentations-4/), containing every possible argument one could make against his positions—is that AI tends to be locked to old categories and, by consequence, traps you inside of them. In his [recent post on the subject](https://x.com/drmichaellevin/status/2114794440896487530) he put it this way:

> *"The problem is that currently, the AI’s seem to be making the same kind of logical errors (from my perspective of course) and expressing the same kind of preconceptions that are prevalent in status quo human thinkers… Right now the LLMs are trapped in the same attractors of thinking that have held several fields back for decades; perhaps not surprisingly. That will change; let’s go AIs—read the papers, follow new directions, be not afraid of ancient philosophical ‘category errors’."*

In that sense, trained on previous corpora of human knowledge and without phenomenological awareness, the AI remains self-referential with that corpus. Of "within" and "without," the AI is forced to be "within," because that is all that it knows.

The more anthropological view on this is that if you come to a tribe that has never touched the outside world, and its "training data" is thusly limited, how can you expect it to interact with and absorb outside knowledge that is outside of its frame? This is the origin of ["cargo cults"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_cult) and various other phenomena that are now well documented.

Consequently, if "dualism" is a position that implies a "without," then AI cannot but collapse the categories and imply that this other is improbable. For example, if math is placed inside of the first category (and thus subject to our full framework of causality), that does not necessarily imply that it is not in some way agentic, or that we fully understand how math works—and, more particularly, how it binds itself to biology, in what cases that constitutes a law, and in what other circumstances it may be more stochastically oriented. That is, after all, exactly the question the hornet wings left open above: Thompson’s sheet is a law on fish bodies and on the outer wing frame, and something looser at the interior cross-vein junctions.

Similarly, we have everywhere the problem of "minds," which Levin and the aforementioned Joscha Bach have discussed with various framings—such as the possibility that various forms of consciousness may be framed as computational and may cross between different computational substrates (Bach’s position), or, in Levin’s own [anti-computationalist framing](https://thoughtforms.life/qa-from-the-internet-and-recent-presentations-4/), that whatever mind a system has lives precisely in the spaces its algorithm *neither forces nor forbids*. In that sense, humans, despite being a relatively advanced species in some respects, have not even quite figured out [how trees communicate with each other](https://doi.org/10.1038/41557). So there is a lot to learn here. A great deal of it is science, but not all of it—which is one reason why new research institutes need to exist.

One particularly helpful arena—covered by the [California Institute for Machine Consciousness](https://cimc.ai) (including in the philosophy seminars I used to organize there), but even more so by folks like the [Qualia Research Institute](https://qri.org), the [Institute for Advanced Consciousness Studies (IACS)](https://advancedconsciousness.org), [Meditation Research at Harvard / MGH](https://meditation.mgh.harvard.edu/), and [Sensoria Research](https://sensoriaresearch.substack.com/)—is what those in our community call the **phenomenological state space**. This concept effectively concerns altered states of consciousness, especially those in which one believes one has encountered an "Other."

Many such examples exist, including dreams, extended meditations, pharmacologically induced states, mantras, and art. What one encounters is sometimes described as "the divine," a particular deity, a machine elf, or a flash of insight—and in many cases the sense of self disappears, such that one appears to be one with this other.

Part of the more general question in adjudicating any such "other" is not necessarily doubting the realness of these encounters, but how they relate to the rest of our lives. In this, the growing field of xenobiology—the study of [forms never yet seen in nature](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1910837117), pioneered by [Levin](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201) and others—has much in common with these other fields, which have mostly bubbled up from the deep reaches of the intellectually and experimentally oriented internet, and they have much to offer each other—even to AI, if the phenomenological reach can also be integrated.²

### The Deep Platonic AI Stack

The problem, if you read two seminal pieces in the AI literature, is even more profound. One prong suggests that we are converging on a single Platonic representation—the **Platonic Representation Hypothesis** of [Huh, Cheung, Wang & Isola (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987), who show that as vision and language models scale, they measure distance between datapoints in more and more alike ways, and hypothesize that this convergence is driving toward "a shared statistical model of reality, akin to Plato’s concept of an ideal reality"—because Platonism is basically correct. There is math that orders the universe, and even when you have an interface layer that is "biology," the messiness is in the bottom layer. In that sense there is a hierarchy, and our physical wetware is not at the top. Even when it is deterministic, it is probably deterministic in the flawed sense of a machine that sometimes brings a task to completion and sometimes forgets what it is doing along the way. We have, in other words, a "good" (Platonic perfection) and a "bad" (messy biology).

The second prong—the **Artificial Hivemind** of [Jiang, Chai, Li, et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954), who put 26,000 open-ended real-world queries to language models and found not only intra-model repetition but, more so, inter-model homogeneity, with different models producing strikingly similar outputs—suggests that all AI is feeding on the same basic dataset, including both the internet and talking to other AIs, and thus converges on endless samehood, aka a hivemind. Back in our phenomenological framing, it means that AI is able to access itself.

Although both of these prongs would seem at first glance mutually exclusive—one converges on the world, the other on itself—at the moment they suffer from a similar flaw. Neither is naturally teleological, and neither allows the system to absorb from the "unknown unknown."

This is interesting because, in the words of Jensen Huang (fantastically quoted on a slide at the [J. Craig Venter Memorial Symposium](https://www.jcvi.org/events/j-craig-venter-memorial-symposium-and-gala) under the heading *"…Biology is the Final Frontier for Engineering"*):

> *"For the very first time in human history, biology has the opportunity to be engineering, not science. When something becomes engineering, it becomes exponentially improving."*

That is the same point: engineering is what exploration looks like once the unknown unknown has been made into something known. The relatively unexplored expanse of the cell—the "mess"—remains one of the best ways to discover this path to knowing, including through the empirical yet data-driven exploration recorded in our evolving Problem Graph (Plate XXII, footnote ³). Every node there is pinned to something outside the corpus—a wet-lab dataset, a held-out species, a hornet wing—so when a pre-registered prediction fails, the failure comes from nature rather than from the hivemind. That is the one exit from both prongs we have found so far: teleology supplied by the locked hypothesis, and the unknown unknown supplied by the organism.

So what does this do to the scoreboard? Nothing to any claim's points, and everything to how a number should be read. The headline itself was later reweighted in public (errata S16); that is a different act from the one this room refuses, which is editing a point by hand. Our audit proved that the dynamics `D` are *sufficient* for every behaviour in the sorting arrays—`0` cell mismatches, `ΔI = −1` on every swap. **Sufficiency of `D` is not falsity of `P`.** "`0.0%` needed" is a statement about what the code requires, not about what exists. An auditor that was `~90%` AI on code, proofs, and refereeing in the technical sections (see the Transparency Heatmap in the Appendix, Plate XXIII) is, by Levin’s own argument, structurally unable to see "without"—so the `0.0%` is a lower bound produced by a reviewer who can only look in one direction. Three of the four dualist deductions survive that objection, because they are deductions for sentences that are false *about the code*: the `FREEZE` handler exists (Claim 1), zero cells cross the barrier (Claim 2), and no swap goes uphill on `I(v)` (Claim 2). The fourth is less clear-cut. Claim 3’s `1.5` "ghost clustering" points were deducted for an **unprogrammed, unpredicted pattern** that turned out to be a mathematical free lunch of the medium—exactly the kind of thing we award `P_structural` credit for in every other room of this museum. Filing it under "ghost" may have been the auditor forcing within/without to be the only two boxes. We do not fix that by hand; a human reaching in to move a point would be the same error in the other direction. We fix it by leaving the loop open.

> **Curator’s verdict — Not an override, a loop.** No one in this museum is allowed to edit a claim's points by hand, and Plate XIX does not: every card still shows the points it had. ## Exit Through the Reading Room — 77.6++% Right on Platonic Ingress³

*Wall label · The Reading Room · Texts: Plato,* Seventh Letter *341c–d; Aristotle,* Historia Animalium *and* De Anima*; Thompson (1910) Oxford translation, (1913) Herbert Spencer Lecture, (1917)* On Growth and Form *· Plate XXI.*

In a private exchange with Levin, I cited [my other article on Hellenic Tantra](https://voegelinview.com/hellenic-tantra-review/) and noted the longstanding Hellenic practice—running through Eleusis, Delphi, and Plato’s own dialogues (*Seventh Letter*, 341c–d)—of a nonverbal, ritualized encounter with other forms of knowing that were not even allowed to be scribbled down. Re-reading D’Arcy Thompson’s *On Growth and Form*, I was struck by the explicit influence of Aristotle’s *History of Animals*—and when I went back to Thompson’s own 1910 Oxford translation and prefatory note to that work (where he shows how Aristotle’s biology grew out of direct observation at a specific place, the lagoon of Pyrrha on Lesbos), I realized that this "direct knowing," rooted in specific places, is what actually grounds and enforces epistemic rigor. It is what allows us to formulate things that are genuine mathematical laws rather than the "would-be" or "almost" laws we so often find in the contemporary literature of emergence. That is also the wisdom of tradition and of standing on the shoulders of giants: Thompson stated his coordinate transformations as conjecture and presumably yearned for the proof—and here we stand, with modern computational tools, holding something that approaches a true law of biological form.

It was also via our method—which deliberately includes the adversarial audit of advanced AI—that we were made aware of Thompson’s 1913 Herbert Spencer Lecture at Oxford, [*On Aristotle as a Biologist*](https://fractastical.github.io/morpholib/thompson_1913_aristotle_as_a_biologist.html) ([Thompson 1913](https://fractastical.github.io/morpholib/thompson_1913_aristotle_as_a_biologist.html)). The lecture makes the bold claim that Aristotle’s power as a philosopher comes from his strong localized knowledge and his initial concentration as a naturalist: the natural history of the lagoon at Pyrrha came *first*, and the metaphysics grew out of it. Thompson then goes further—the concept of the *psyche* (what we have since come to call the "soul") is itself derived from the observation of nature. Aristotle’s *De Anima* classifies the soul the way he classified fishes: a nutritive soul shared with plants, a sentient and motile soul shared with animals, and a reasoning soul in man, each lower grade implicit in the higher "as a triangle within a square." It is a nested geometry, not a ghost; and in *De Anima* II.4 that same psyche is named as the body’s formal, efficient, and final cause at once—the very analysis of causation that made Aristotle’s precise and elaborate taxonomies possible, and that still underlies many of our maps of nature. In that sense, if we want to understand what animates us, we may need to start not with the later, transmuted, and oversimplified idea of the soul, but with the original one: a graded, nested form, read off living bodies by a man who had spent his years dissecting cuttlefish on Lesbos.
