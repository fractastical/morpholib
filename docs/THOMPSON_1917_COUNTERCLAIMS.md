## Per-claim literature verdict (all claims: Thompson, 1917)

### T1917-001 — Thompson (1917), Ch. I, pp. 1-15, Figs. - — `NOT_TESTABLE`

**Claim (1917):** The form of an organism is a 'diagram of forces': form can and should be described physically and mathematically, in terms of the forces that produced it.

**Literature (Gould (1971); Medawar and later commentators):** Gould, S.J. (1971) 'D'Arcy Thompson and the science of form' (essay; repr. in Gould 1977, Ever Since Darwin)

**Counterclaim / verdict:** No counterclaim is possible against a programme. Gould's influential qualification: physical forces are real but form is also shaped by evolutionary history and contingency -- 'form as diagram of forces' is incomplete, not false.

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `NOT_TESTABLE_AS_STATED` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-002 — Thompson (1917), Ch. I + Epilogue, pp. 1-15; Epilogue, Figs. - — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** Many forms are explained by physical forces directly and do not require (and are not well explained by) adaptation/heredity acting part-by-part; related forms differ by whole-form transformation rather than by independent change of parts.

**Literature (Gould (1971, and from the 1980s)):** Gould (1971); Gould & Lewontin (1979) Proc. R. Soc. B 205:581-598 (spandrels paper)

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Thompson's anti-adaptationist, whole-form programme was first celebrated (Gould 1971, which revived him), then qualified by Gould himself: from the 1980s he refuted Thompson's mechanical determinism in favour of historical contingency and 'tinkering'. Modern synthesis: constraints (Thompson's half) and selection both operate; neither alone explains form.

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `PARTIAL_REPO` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-003 — Thompson (1917), Ch. II, pp. 16-49, Figs. 1-2 — `PARTIALLY_REFUTED`

**Claim (1917):** Surface grows as L^2 and volume/weight as L^3, so surface:volume falls as 1/L; this alone bounds organism size and forces disproportionate change of parts with size.

**Literature (Kleiber (1932); Glazier (2022)):** Kleiber, M. (1932) Hilgardia 6:315-353; Glazier, D.S. (2022) Proc. R. Soc. B 289:20221605

**Counterclaim / verdict:** The surface-law application (metabolism proportional to surface, M^2/3) was countered by Kleiber (1932): basal metabolic rate scales as ~M^3/4, not M^2/3. Glazier (2022) counters both: exponents are variable and context-dependent -- there is no universal 2/3 or 3/4. The pure geometry (area L^2, volume L^3) is not in dispute; its use as a metabolic law is.

**Quantitative margin:** Exponent dispute: 0.67 (surface law) vs 0.73-0.75 (Kleiber 1932) vs 'no universal exponent' (Glazier 2022)

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-004 — Thompson (1917), Ch. II, pp. 16-49, Figs. - — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** Strength of a support scales with cross-sectional area (L^2) while load scales with weight (L^3), so larger land animals must be relatively thicker-boned / differently proportioned (Galilean scaling).

**Literature (McMahon (1973); Schmidt-Nielsen (1984) and later allometry literature):** McMahon, T.A. (1973) Science 179:1201-1204 (elastic similarity); Schmidt-Nielsen, K. (1984) Scaling: Why Is Animal Size So Important?

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Pure geometric similarity (strength L^2 vs weight L^3) does not describe real skeletons; McMahon's elastic-similarity (buckling) criterion predicts different, often better-fitting proportions. Thompson's scaling dilemma is real, his implied geometric solution is not the one animals use in general.

**Quantitative margin:** Elastic similarity predicts diameter proportional to length^1.5 (vs geometric proportionality); fits many trees/limbs better, neither fits all

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-005 — Thompson (1917), Ch. II, pp. 16-49, Figs. 2 — `SUPPORTED`

**Claim (1917):** Different physics dominates at different magnitudes: surface tension and viscosity rule small organisms, gravity and inertia rule large ones; there is a least size of organism set by molecular/thermal scales.

**Literature (Vogel (1994) and the low-Reynolds-number literature):** Vogel, S. (1994) Life in Moving Fluids, 2nd ed.; Purcell (1977) Am. J. Phys. 45:3-11

**Counterclaim / verdict:** No substantive counterclaim. Purcell (1977) made Thompson's small-scale regime famous ('life at low Reynolds number'): viscosity, not inertia, rules the small -- exactly Thompson's magnitude argument, quantified.

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-006 — Thompson (1917), Ch. II, pp. 16-49, Figs. 1 — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** Cell (e.g. nerve-cell) size is roughly independent of the size of the animal; larger animals have more cells, not proportionally larger cells.

**Literature (Modern cell-size literature (e.g. Marshall et al. 2012)):** Marshall, W.F. et al. (2012) Curr. Biol. 22:R256-R260 ('What determines cell size?')

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Broadly right, with named exceptions Thompson did not treat: some neurons, eggs, syncytia and polyploid cells scale with organism/ploidy. Cell size is actively regulated, not a passive constant.

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-007 — Thompson (1917), Ch. III, pp. 50-155, Figs. 3-40 — `SUPERSEDED`

**Claim (1917):** Growth in size/weight over time follows a definite, plottable curve: rapid (near-compound-interest) early growth with a steadily diminishing specific rate of growth.

**Literature (von Bertalanffy (1957)):** von Bertalanffy, L. (1957) Q. Rev. Biol. 32:217-231

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Thompson's plotted growth curves are descriptive. Von Bertalanffy replaced curve-description with a mechanistic balance law (dW/dt = anabolism - catabolism) that predicts the curve family. Later qualification: the 'universal' 2/3 exponent in that law is not universal -- optimally fitted exponents vary widely; the von Bertalanffy function is 'weakly universal' for fish, not for non-fish (PeerJ 2018, 'On the exponent in the Von Bertalanffy growth model').

**Quantitative margin:** Superseded as method, not refuted as data: no single growth curve/exponent fits all taxa

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-008 — Thompson (1917), Ch. III, pp. 50-155, Figs. 18-22 — `MIXED`

**Claim (1917):** Parts of an organism grow at different but definite relative rates, so form changes systematically with size; the changing proportions of organs (brain, heart, body; plaice weight-length) are growth-rate phenomena.

**Literature (Huxley (1932) formalised it; Royal Society discussion (1950) and Gould (1966) qualified it):** Huxley, J.S. (1932) Problems of Relative Growth; Needham, Zuckerman et al. (1950) Proc. R. Soc. B 137:465-525; Gould, S.J. (1966) Biol. Rev. 41:587-640

**Counterclaim / verdict:** SUPPORTING: Huxley (1932) turned Thompson's differential-growth claim into the allometric law y = b x^k. COUNTER (1950): the allometry equation cannot simultaneously hold between two parts and the whole, and often only fits as piecewise straight lines -- so its constants are 'a rough and ready shorthand of description', not physiological entities. Fitting the law does not prove a growth-rate mechanism.

**Quantitative margin:** Huxley's k = ratio of specific growth rates; the 1950 objection is logical (inconsistent between part-part and part-whole fits), not a percentage

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_REPO` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-011 — Thompson (1917), Ch. IV, pp. 156-200, Figs. 41-59 — `SUPERSEDED`

**Claim (1917):** The mitotic figure behaves as a system of forces between two poles: spindle and aster configurations, and chromosome movements, follow the lines of a bipolar field of force (with Peddie's magnetic-field diagrams as the physical analogue).

**Literature (Mitchison & Kirschner (1984); McIntosh (2012)):** Mitchison, T. & Kirschner, M. (1984) Nature 312:237-242 (dynamic instability / search-and-capture); McIntosh, J.R. (2012) 'Motors or dynamics: what really moves chromosomes?'

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Thompson/Peddie's bipolar field-of-force mechanism is not the mechanism. The spindle is a self-organising microtubule machine: dynamic instability (1984) lets microtubules search-and-capture chromosomes, and depolymerisation itself generates poleward force with no motor and no ATP (shown in vitro from 1988). The bipolar *geometry* Thompson drew was right; the force-field physics was replaced.

**Quantitative margin:** Mechanism replaced wholesale (1984-2012); geometry retained

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-013 — Thompson (1917), Ch. V, pp. 201-276, Figs. 60-68 — `PARTIALLY_REFUTED`

**Claim (1917):** A free, isolated cell (like a liquid drop) tends to the spherical/minimal-area form under surface tension, departing from it only under identifiable constraints.

**Literature (Heer et al. / Lecuit-group reviews (2017); Fischer-Friedrich et al. (2014)):** 'Tension, contraction and tissue morphogenesis' (2017, PMC5769629), Box 1 'A note about surface tension'

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Directly addressed to Thompson (1917): 'While D'Arcy Thompson dismissed the connection between surface tension and contractility, we now understand that this connection is paramount... the tension at cell surfaces is NOT driven by surface tension, as Thompson understood it, but by tension in the cortical actin layer.' Membrane tension's contribution to effective surface tension is often negligible. The sphere-as-null survives; Thompson's passive-liquid mechanism does not.

**Quantitative margin:** Mechanism refuted; spherical null shape retained as approximation

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-014 — Thompson (1917), Ch. V, pp. 201-276, Figs. 61-67, 72-96 — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** The forms of single cells and cell-like organisms (Plateau's oil globules; Lagena and other forams/protists) are surfaces of revolution from the minimal-surface catalogue: sphere, cylinder, unduloid, nodoid, catenoid.

**Literature (Modern cell-mechanics comparisons (e.g. PLOS Comput. Biol. 2023, cell shape vs minimal surfaces)):** 'Modelling cell shape in 3D structured environments' (2023) PLOS Comput. Biol. 19:e1011412

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Constant-mean-curvature (Plateau/Delaunay) shapes are a good model for suspended cells and a measurably wrong one for adherent/structured cells: observed-vs-minimised shape discrepancy is large in L- and V-shaped micropatterns (spectral amplitude difference ~0.15-0.18) and small in triangular patterns (~0.03). Thompson's catalogue is a limiting case, not the general rule.

**Quantitative margin:** Discrepancy ~0.15-0.18 (L/V patterns) vs ~0.03 (triangular): context decides, contra a universal surface-tension law

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_REPO` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-018 — Thompson (1917), Ch. VII, pp. 293-345, Figs. 100-135 — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** Cells in contact, like soap films, meet three-at-a-time at ~120 degrees and in other respects obey Plateau's laws of film configuration.

### T1917-019 — Thompson (1917), Ch. VII, pp. 293-345, Figs. 120-127 — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** Tissue cells tessellate approximately as hexagons / as a froth, the configuration that partitions area with least perimeter.

**Literature (Gibson et al. (2006); Farhadifar et al. (2007)):** Gibson, M.C. et al. (2006) Nature 442:1038-1041; Farhadifar et al. (2007)

### T1917-021 — Thompson (1917), Ch. VIII, pp. 346-410, Figs. 136-193 — `PARTIALLY_REFUTED`

**Claim (1917):** The bee's honeycomb - hexagonal prisms closed by three rhombi meeting at the Maraldi angle (~109 deg 28 min) - is the configuration using least wax for the capacity (the isoperimetric claim).

**Literature (Fejes Toth (1964); Hales (2001)):** Fejes Toth, L. (1964) Bull. AMS 70:468-481, 'What the bees know and what they do not know'; Hales, T.C. (2001) Ann. of Math. 153 (honeycomb conjecture proved)

**Counterclaim / verdict:** SPLIT VERDICT, and the split is the famous one. (a) The hexagonal tiling IS optimal -- proved by Hales (2001): Thompson/Pappus right. (b) The bees' three-rhombi cell base is NOT the wax-minimum: Fejes Toth (1964) built a better base (two hexagons + two rhombi). Thompson's 'least wax' claim is therefore wrong in its 3D detail. Fejes Toth himself adds that the saving is practically illusory.

**Quantitative margin:** Better base saves <0.35% of the area of a cell opening (much less of total cell surface) -- Fejes Toth (1964); a 2025 re-optimisation puts the total available saving at ~0.50%

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_ELSEWHERE` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-027 — Thompson (1917), Ch. XI, pp. 493-586, Figs. 237-303 — `QUALIFIED`

**Claim (1917):** Spiral shells (and other gnomonic-growth structures) are logarithmic (equiangular) spirals: the wharves enlarge by a constant ratio, so the form is unchanged by growth (r proportional to e^{k theta}).

**Literature (Raup (1966)):** Raup, D.M. (1966) J. Paleontol. 40:1178-1190; Raup & Michelson (1965) Science 147

**Counterclaim / verdict:** Raup did not refute the logarithmic spiral; he demoted it from law to coordinate system. Real shells need three parameters (W whorl expansion, D, T) plus a generating-curve shape, parameters can drift during growth, and large regions of the possible morphospace are empty -- which a pure log-spiral law does not explain. Modern spiral-fitting (2021, 'Refined methods for estimating spirals', doi:10.26879/1071) needs 12 spiral types and treats 'spiral deviation' as the informative signal: exact log-spiral growth is the null, not the finding.

**Quantitative margin:** 3+ Raup parameters replace Thompson's single spiral constant; deviations from log growth are systematic, not noise

**Repo 2026 (not literature):** status `DONE_REPO` -- see claim inventory.

### T1917-038 — Thompson (1917), Ch. XVI, pp. 670-718, Figs. 331-334, 336-352 — `SUPERSEDED`

### T1917-039 — Thompson (1917), Ch. XVII, pp. 719-776, Figs. 353-408 — `PARTIALLY_REFUTED`

**Claim (1917):** GENERAL THESIS: the form of a related species/genus can be obtained from another by a simple, continuous deformation of the whole coordinate system (a transformation of the whole form), rather than by unrelated changes of separate parts.

**Literature (Bookstein (1977, 1978, 1989, and again 2023); Scholtz, Knotel & Baum (2020)):** Bookstein, F.L. (1977) Math. Biosci. 34:177-219; (1978) Comput. Biol. Med.; (1989) IEEE TPAMI 11:567-585; (2023) Evol. Biol.; Scholtz et al. (2020) Zoomorphology 139 (doi:10.1007/s00435-020-00494-1)

## Rules for using this document

1. Date both sides, always: 'Thompson (1917) claimed X; **Fejes Toth (1964)** showed Y (<0.35%)'. An undated 'Thompson was wrong' is not a citation.
2. `SUPERSEDED` is not `REFUTED`: Murray (1926), von Bertalanffy (1957) and Bookstein (1989) replaced Thompson's *method* while keeping much of his question. Say which.
3. `NO_DIRECT_TEST` (30 claims) means the literature has *not* spoken -- including several famous figures (primate skulls, horse skull, horns, forams). Do not let a neighbouring result (e.g. Bookstein on grids generally) masquerade as a test of a specific pair.
4. Never cite REPO 2026 numbers as literature. The *Scarus*->*Pomacanthus* refutation is ours (2026) precisely because the literature left it untested for 109 years.
5. Where the literature counters itself (trabeculae 1987 vs 2007; eggs 2017 vs 2019; TPS 1989 vs Bookstein 2023), record both rounds -- the second counterclaim is usually the more interesting one.
