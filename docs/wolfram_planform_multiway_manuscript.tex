\begin{abstract}
When a planarian flatworm or an amphibian/arthropod limb is surgically cut or bioelectrically perturbed, it can regenerate a striking variety of abnormal body plans: two heads, three heads, lateral side-heads sprouting from the belly, or a second head growing directly out of the tip of an existing head. In two foundational papers, Lobo, Levin, and colleagues introduced \textbf{PlanformDB~2.5.0} (Lobo, Malone \& Levin 2013, encoding $369$ planarian morphologies across $1{,}181$ regions) and \textbf{LimbformDB~2.1.0} (Lobo et al.\ 2014, encoding $555$ salamander, frog, crustacean, insect, and arachnid limbs across $3{,}146$ joints) to store these experimental outcomes as formal graphs. However, both databases remained \emph{static descriptive catalogs}, leaving three foundational problems open: (i)~\emph{The Graph Equivalence Problem}---how many distinct body-plan wiring diagrams actually exist in the databases up to exact graph isomorphism, and when does simple part-counting fail? (ii)~\emph{The Generative Grammar Problem}---what is the minimal set of formal graph-rewrite rules needed to grow $100\%$ of the observed morphologies from cut tissue fragments? and (iii)~\emph{The Combinatorial Search Problem}---how can an automated theory-discovery engine search millions of multi-step growth trajectories without drowning in isomorphic node-numbering duplicates?

Here we solve all three problems by formulating planarian and limb regeneration as a \textbf{Multiway Double-Pushout (DPO) Hypergraph Rewrite System}, coupling the standalone C++20 \texttt{hg\_evolve} engine from the Wolfram Physics Project (\texttt{WolframInstitute/HypergraphRewritingEngine}, \texttt{v1.0.0-rc2}; Assar 2026) directly to \texttt{PlanformDB~2.5.0} and \texttt{LimbformDB~2.1.0} over Wolfram Exchange Format (WXF), and verifying the governing algebraic laws in the \textbf{Lean~4} theorem prover (\texttt{0 sorry}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Why Counting Body Parts Fails (The $38$ Planform Classes \& $7$ Hidden Topological Isomers):} Just as butane and isobutane share the chemical formula $\mathrm{C}_4\mathrm{H}_{10}$ (`4 Carbons, 10 Hydrogens') while differing in branched vs.\ linear wiring, counting planarian regions $(\#\text{Head}, \#\text{Trunk}, \#\text{Tail}, \#\text{Links})$ lumps together fundamentally different body plans. Running exact McKay individualization--refinement (IR) canonicalization (\texttt{ir\_core.hpp}) compresses the $369$ \texttt{PlanformDB} rows into \textbf{$38$ exact region-link isomorphism classes} ($9.71\times$ redundancy; $126$ classes with organs) and the $555$ \texttt{LimbformDB} rows into \textbf{$51$ segment-tree classes} ($10.88\times$ redundancy; $92$ with digits), uncovering \textbf{$7$ hidden topological isomers} ($31$ part-count buckets $\to 38$ IR classes). For example, worms with $(2\text{ Heads}, 1\text{ Trunk}, 1\text{ Tail}, 3\text{ Links})$ split into three non-isomorphic body plans: radial Trunk-hub stars (\texttt{\#3 Side head}, $n=12$, Zagreb index $Z_1 = 12$), Head-hub stars (\texttt{\#112}, $n=1$), and linear chains (\texttt{\#119 Head on head}, $n=2$, $Z_1 = 10$).
  \item \textbf{All $369$ Planarians Are Generated by Just Three Primitive Rules ($\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$):} At first glance, generating all $38$ \texttt{PlanformDB} classes appears to require $8$ domain-specific rules (\texttt{R1--R6} terminal blastema sprouting, which covers $33/38$ classes, plus \texttt{R7--R8} intercalary bridging for internal heads/tails). We prove that all $8$ rules reduce algebraically to \textbf{three primitive DPO rules}: \textbf{one universal budding rule} $L_1: [[0]] \to [[0],[1],[0,1,0,1],[1,0,1,0]]$ (grow an undifferentiated blastema bud---the \emph{exact same single rule} that generates all \texttt{LimbformDB} trees) and \textbf{two bioelectric fate switches} $D_1: [[0]] \to [[0,0]]$ ($\text{Blastema} \to \text{Head}$) and $D_2: [[0]] \to [[0,0,0]]$ ($\text{Blastema} \to \text{Tail}$). Because $D_1$ and $D_2$ consume the pluripotent tag $[[0]]$, terminal differentiation locks a region against further budding; ``intercalary'' worms (\texttt{\#119}, \texttt{\#282}) require no special rule, emerging automatically whenever a blank bud branches twice via $L_1$ \emph{before} its bioelectric switch ($D_1, D_2$) locks it. Stripping region colors projects all $369$ worms onto just \textbf{$12$ unlabelled skeleton topologies} ($278/369 = 75.3\%$ on the single 3-node path $P_3$).
  \item \textbf{$44{,}534\times$ Quotient Exploration Speedup on \texttt{LimbformDB} (\texttt{OEIS A000055} in $85\text{ ms}$):} Growing a limb tree up to $V = 12$ joints step by step produces $43{,}954{,}714$ raw factorial trajectories ($\sum k!$) that differ only by node numbering. Using \texttt{ExploreFromCanonicalStatesOnly}, \texttt{hg\_evolve} expands only the \textbf{$987$ unique unlabelled trees} (\texttt{OEIS A000055}) while analytically reconstructing all $43.95\text{ million}$ raw states via \texttt{quotient\_multiplicity\_core.hpp} in \textbf{$85\text{ ms}$} ($44{,}533.65\times$ compression), matching \textbf{$35/35$ ($100.0\%$)} of \texttt{LimbformDB} classes with $\le 12$ joints ($532/555 = 95.86\%$ of the database).
  \item \textbf{Bioelectric \texttt{RuleWeights} Gating \& Machine-Checked Lean~4 Proofs:} Coupling the multiway posterior rule weights $(w_{D_1}, w_{D_2})$ to electrotonic gap-junction decay across the $5$ anterior--posterior crop positions of Oviedo et al.\ (2010) reproduces the transition from Wild-Type (\texttt{\#1}, $1\text{H}1\text{T}$) to Bipolar Double-Head (\texttt{\#2}, $2\text{H}0\text{T}$) on 1-octanol Series S1 ($r = 0.9823$) and held-out innexin-RNAi Series S2 ($r = 0.9088$). All four structural DPO, Zagreb-isomer, quotient, and reducibility laws are machine-checked in Lean~4 (\texttt{Loop\_wolfram\_planform\_multiway.lean}, \texttt{0 sorry}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Introduction: Three Open Problems in the Bioinformatics of Regeneration}

When Daniel Lobo and Michael Levin published the \textbf{Planform}~\cite{Lobo2013Planform,Lobo2013BiologyOpen} and \textbf{Limbform}~\cite{Lobo2014Limbform,Lobo2014Regeneration} databases, they framed their research program as \emph{``Towards a bioinformatics of patterning''} and \emph{``A bioinformatics expert system linking functional data to anatomical outcomes.''} What those landmark papers built was the \textbf{descriptive graph encoding}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PlanformDB~2.5.0}~\cite{Lobo2013Planform,Lobo2013BiologyOpen}: $369$ planarian morphologies (\emph{Dugesia japonica} and \emph{Schmidtea mediterranea}) encoded across $1{,}181$ regions (\texttt{Head}, \texttt{Trunk}, \texttt{Tail}), border links, and internal organs (\texttt{Eye}, \texttt{Brain}, \texttt{Pharynx}, \texttt{VNC}).
  \item \textbf{LimbformDB~2.1.0}~\cite{Lobo2014Limbform,Lobo2014Regeneration}: $555$ limb morphologies across amphibians (axolotl, newt, \emph{Xenopus}), crustaceans, insects, and arachnids encoded across $3{,}146$ joints, segment links, \texttt{LinkSubJoint} ectopic grafts, and digits.
\end{itemize}

However, both databases remained static lookup tables of 2D cartoons. Three fundamental mathematical and computational problems were left open for the field:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Open Problem 1 --- The Graph Equivalence \& Isomer Problem (\emph{``How many distinct body plans are in the database, and why does part-counting fail?''}):} Every time an experiment produced a worm with slightly different region dimensions, eye angles, or SQLite vertex IDs (\texttt{Region.Id = 1, 2, 3...}), a new \texttt{Morphology} entry was created. Neither database included an exact graph-isomorphism canonicalizer to determine which entries share the exact same topological wiring diagram, or to distinguish \textbf{topological isomers}---worms that have the \emph{exact same} number of Heads, Trunks, Tails, and Links, but are wired into a branched star (\texttt{\#3 Side head}) vs.\ a linear chain (\texttt{\#119 Head on head}).
  \item \textbf{Open Problem 2 --- The Minimal Generative Grammar Problem (\emph{``How many rewrite rules does nature actually need to build all $369$ worms and $555$ limbs?''}):} \texttt{PlanformDB} stores the surgical cut (\emph{before}) and the final morphology 14 days later (\emph{after}), but contains zero formal graph-rewrite rules connecting them. Later reaction--diffusion and bioelectric models~\cite{Cervera2020,Cervera2021,Oviedo2010,Beane2011} modeled only a 1D line ($1\text{H}1\text{T}$ vs.\ $2\text{H}0\text{T}$), which cannot represent branched worms (\texttt{\#3}, \texttt{\#56}) or intercalary worms (\texttt{\#119}, \texttt{\#282}).
  \item \textbf{Open Problem 3 --- The Combinatorial Explosion Problem in Automated Theory Discovery (\emph{``How do we search multi-step growth rules without $k!$ blowup?''}):} Growing a 12-joint limb tree step by step generates $11! = 39{,}916{,}800$ raw trajectories at step 11 alone ($43{,}954{,}714$ cumulative raw states) because naive simulators treat differently numbered copies of the same tree as distinct states.
\end{enumerate}

In this paper, we resolve all three open problems by integrating the standalone C++20 \texttt{hg\_evolve} engine from the Wolfram Physics Project (\texttt{WolframInstitute/HypergraphRewritingEngine}, \texttt{v1.0.0-rc2}; Assar 2026~\cite{Assar2026,Wolfram2020}) into the \textbf{Infinite Morphospace / MorphoEval} verification pipeline~\cite{Dietz2026MorphoEval} over Wolfram Exchange Format (WXF), backed by machine-checked proofs in \textbf{Lean~4} (\texttt{0 sorry}).

\section{Hypergraph Encoding \& Exact McKay IR Isomorphism Census}

\subsection{Encoding Typed Anatomical Graphs as Ordered Hypergraphs}

In the Lobo--Levin formalism~\cite{Lobo2013BiologyOpen,Lobo2014Regeneration}, a planarian morphology $M = (V, E_{\text{link}}, E_{\text{organ}})$ is a finite graph whose vertices $v \in V$ carry a region type $\tau(v) \in \{\mathtt{Trunk}, \mathtt{Head}, \mathtt{Tail}\}$, connected by undirected anatomical adjacency links $\{u, v\} \in E_{\text{link}}$. Because \texttt{HypergraphRewritingEngine} operates on multisets of ordered hyperedges of arity $1 \le a \le 16$ (\texttt{SPEC.md} \S1) and preserves arity and within-edge vertex order under McKay IR canonicalization (\texttt{ir\_core.hpp}), we encode vertex types and edge types into disjoint arity signatures without auxiliary label tables:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathtt{Trunk}(v) &\;\mapsto\; [v] \quad (\text{arity } 1), &
    \mathtt{Head}(v) &\;\mapsto\; [v, v] \quad (\text{arity } 2 \text{ self-loop}), \\
    \mathtt{Tail}(v) &\;\mapsto\; [v, v, v] \quad (\text{arity } 3 \text{ self-loop}), &
    \mathtt{Link}\{u, v\} &\;\mapsto\; [u, v, u, v],\, [v, u, v, u] \quad (\text{symmetric arity } 4).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:encoding}
\end{equation}
When organ landmarks are included, each organ attached to region $v$ allocates a fresh leaf vertex $w$ with organ-specific arity $\mathtt{Eye} \mapsto 5$, $\mathtt{Brain} \mapsto 6$, $\mathtt{Pharynx} \mapsto 7$, and $\mathtt{VNC} \mapsto 8$. For \texttt{LimbformDB~2.1.0}, joints are encoded as arity-1 hyperedges $[j]$, segments as symmetric arity-4 link pairs (splitting host segments along any attached \texttt{LinkSubJoint} ectopic graft sequence $j_{\text{from}} \to j_{\text{sub},1} \to \dots \to j_{\text{to}}$ so ectopic branches are topologically connected to their host segment), and distal digits as arity-3 hyperedges $[j_{\text{to}}, d, d]$.

\section{The Generative DPO Grammar and Its Three-Rule Reducibility Hierarchy}

\subsection{Apparent Domain-Specific Grammar ($8$ Rules Across $3$ Tiers)}

At the phenomenological level of surgical blastema formation, planarian regeneration in \texttt{PlanformDB} appears to require $8$ domain-specific DPO rules grouped into two connected tiers plus a bisected-trunk seed (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}C):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Terminal Blastema Sprouting, \texttt{R1--R6}):} A \texttt{Trunk} sprouts a \texttt{Head} (\texttt{R1}), \texttt{Tail} (\texttt{R2}), or \texttt{Trunk} (\texttt{R3}); or an excised \texttt{Head}/\texttt{Tail} fragment directly sprouts a \texttt{Tail} (\texttt{R4}, \texttt{R6}) or \texttt{Head} (\texttt{R5}). Evolving \texttt{R1--R6} for $6$ steps generates $2{,}738$ canonical states ($169{,}440$ events) and recovers \textbf{$33/38$ canonical classes ($361/369 = 97.83\%$ of all worms in \texttt{PlanformDB})}.
  \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Intercalary Bridge Sprouting, \texttt{R7--R8}):} Adding \texttt{R7} ($\mathtt{Head} \to \mathtt{Head} + \mathtt{Trunk}$) and \texttt{R8} ($\mathtt{Tail} \to \mathtt{Tail} + \mathtt{Trunk}$) recovers the $4$ remaining connected intercalary/grafted classes (\texttt{\#113}, \texttt{\#116}, \texttt{\#282}, \texttt{\#286}), bringing connected coverage to \textbf{$37/38$ classes ($368/369 = 99.73\%$)}.
  \item \textbf{Tier 3 (Bisected Split-Worm Seed):} Including the 2-component excised trunk seed $[[0], [1]]$ recovers \texttt{\#394 Lysis, split worm}, achieving \textbf{$38/38$ ($100.0\%$) exact McKay IR hash recovery}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exact Reduction to the Three-Rule Primitive Basis $\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$}

Why do so many domain-specific rules exist, and are they independent? By inspecting the DPO edge-replacement semantics of \texttt{HypergraphRewritingEngine} (\texttt{SPEC.md} \S2: a rule consumes its LHS hyperedges while preserving all matched vertices and all non-matched incident hyperedges), we prove that \textbf{all $8$ rules \texttt{R1--R8} reduce algebraically to just $3$ primitive DPO rules}:
\begin{align}
  L_1\ (\texttt{L1\_UniversalSprout}): &\quad [[0]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[0],\, [1],\, [0,1,0,1],\, [1,0,1,0]] \quad (\text{pluripotent blastema budding}), \label{eq:L1}\\
  D_1\ (\texttt{D1\_DiffHead}): &\quad [[0]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[0, 0]] \quad (\text{in-place anterior specification: } \mathtt{Trunk/Blastema} \to \mathtt{Head}), \label{eq:D1}\\
  D_2\ (\texttt{D2\_DiffTail}): &\quad [[0]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[0, 0, 0]] \quad (\text{in-place posterior specification: } \mathtt{Trunk/Blastema} \to \mathtt{Tail}). \label{eq:D2}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic Factorization \& Causal Non-Commutativity of $\mathcal{B}_3$]
Every domain-specific rule $\mathtt{R1}\dots\mathtt{R8}$ is a composite multiway path in $\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Terminal Sprouting vs.\ Intercalary Bridging Is Purely Causal Ordering:} Because $D_1$ and $D_2$ consume the unary hyperedge $[[0]]$, any vertex that differentiates into $\mathtt{Head}$ ($[v,v]$) or $\mathtt{Tail}$ ($[v,v,v]$) is \textbf{terminally locked} against further budding by $L_1$. Applying $L_1(0 \to 1)$ followed by $D_1(1)$ yields terminal head sprouting ($\mathtt{R1} = D_1(1) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$, cost $2$), whereas applying $L_1(0 \to 1)$ followed by $D_1(0)$ on a vertex $0$ that already has a link yields intercalary head bridging ($\mathtt{R7} = D_1(0) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$, cost $2$). Similarly, $\mathtt{R2} = D_2(1) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$, $\mathtt{R8} = D_2(0) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$, $\mathtt{R4} = D_2(1) \circ D_1(0) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$ (cost $3$), $\mathtt{R5} = D_1(1) \circ D_1(0) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$ (cost $3$), and $\mathtt{R6} = D_2(1) \circ D_2(0) \circ L_1(0 \to 1)$ (cost $3$).
