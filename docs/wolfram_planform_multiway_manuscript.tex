\begin{abstract}
When a planarian flatworm or an amphibian/arthropod limb is surgically cut or bioelectrically perturbed, it can regenerate a wide variety of abnormal body plans: two heads, three heads, lateral side-heads sprouting from the trunk, or a second head growing directly out of the tip of an existing head. In two foundational papers, Lobo, Levin, and colleagues introduced \textbf{PlanformDB~2.5.0} (Lobo, Malone \& Levin 2013, encoding $369$ planarian morphologies across $1{,}181$ regions) and \textbf{LimbformDB~2.1.0} (Lobo et al.\ 2014, encoding $555$ salamander, frog, crustacean, insect, and arachnid limbs across $3{,}146$ joints) to store these experimental outcomes as formal graphs, comparing shapes via heuristic graph edit distance (GED).

Here we couple the standalone C++20 \texttt{hg\_evolve} multiway double-pushout (DPO) hypergraph rewriting engine from the Wolfram Physics Project (\texttt{WolframInstitute/HypergraphRewritingEngine}, \texttt{v1.0.0-rc2}; Assar 2026) directly to \texttt{PlanformDB~2.5.0} and \texttt{LimbformDB~2.1.0} over Wolfram Exchange Format (WXF), accompanied by machine-checked arithmetic lemmas in \textbf{Lean~4} (\texttt{0 sorry}) and an independent cross-provider referee audit (\texttt{claude-fable-5-1-thinking-high}). We report four principal findings:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exact McKay IR Isomorphism Census ($369 \to 38 \to 12$ in \texttt{PlanformDB}; $555 \to 51$ in \texttt{LimbformDB}) \& Seven Isomer Separations Beyond Count Vectors:} Every one of the $369$ region-encoded morphologies in \texttt{PlanformDB~2.5.0} ($372$ rows minus $3$ regionless entries) is a forest of $\le 7$ regions ($368$ connected trees plus $1$ two-component split worm \texttt{\#394}). Running exact McKay individualization--refinement (IR) canonicalization (\texttt{ir\_core.hpp}) compresses the $369$ rows into \textbf{$38$ exact region-link isomorphism classes} ($9.71\times$ catalog redundancy; $12$ unlabelled forest skeletons, with $278/369 = 75.3\%$ sitting on the single 3-node path $P_3$; $126$ classes when organs are attached) and compresses the $555$ \texttt{LimbformDB} rows into \textbf{$51$ segment-tree classes} ($10.88\times$ redundancy; $92$ with digits). Compared against 4-tuple region-count summaries $(\#\text{Head}, \#\text{Trunk}, \#\text{Tail}, \#\text{Links})$ ($31$ buckets), McKay IR canonicalization resolves \textbf{$7$ extra isomer classes} across $5$ collision groups: \textbf{$3$ skeletal star-vs.-path pairs} (\texttt{\#3} vs.\ \texttt{\#119}, \texttt{\#12} vs.\ \texttt{\#128}, \texttt{\#77} vs.\ \texttt{\#283}, with First Zagreb indices $Z_1 = 12$ vs.\ $10$) and \textbf{$4$ positional/spider isomers} (\texttt{\#112} $\mathtt{H\text{--}Tr\text{--}H\text{--}Ta}$ vs.\ \texttt{\#119} $\mathtt{H\text{--}H\text{--}Tr\text{--}Ta}$, both $P_4$ with $Z_1 = 10$; \texttt{\#229} spider $Z_1 = 16$ vs.\ $P_5$ paths \texttt{\#116} and \texttt{\#282}, $Z_1 = 14$; and $P_5$ paths \texttt{\#113} vs.\ \texttt{\#286}, $Z_1 = 14$).
  \item \textbf{Recall vs.\ Precision of Generative DPO Rewrite Grammars (Syntactic State Space vs.\ Biological Selection):} From the three excised fragment seeds $\{[[0]], [[0,0]], [[0,0,0]]\}$, a 6-rule terminal-sprouting DPO grammar (\texttt{R1--R6}, Tier~1) generates $2{,}738$ canonical states at depth $6$, recovering \textbf{$33/38$ classes ($86.8\%$ recall, $361/369 = 97.8\%$ of worms)} at \textbf{$1.21\%$ precision} ($33/2{,}738$). Adding two intercalary rules (\texttt{R7--R8}, Tier~2) generates $6{,}787$ states at depth $6$, recovering \textbf{$37/38$ connected classes ($97.4\%$ recall, $368/369 = 99.7\%$ of worms)} at \textbf{$0.55\%$ precision} ($37/6{,}787$), with the bisected seed $[[0],[1]]$ recovering \texttt{\#394} ($38/38$). Because all \texttt{PlanformDB} graphs are 3-colored forests, a 3-rule primitive leaf-addition and recoloring basis $\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$ is a \textbf{universal generator of all 3-colored forests}: it reaches $22/38$ classes at depth $6$ ($817$ states, $2.69\%$ precision) and $37/38$ classes at depth $12$ ($754{,}520$ states, \textbf{$0.0049\%$ precision}; depth $13$ required for 7-tail \texttt{\#382} exceeds memory). This cleanly separates the \emph{expressive syntactic substrate} (which compresses nothing on its own) from the \emph{biological/bioelectric regulatory mechanism} $D$ that selects the observed target morphologies out of $\sim 10^5\text{--}10^6$ reachable trees.
  \item \textbf{$44{,}534\times$ Labelled-to-Unlabelled State-Space Reduction on \texttt{OEIS A000055} \& \texttt{LimbformDB}:} Evolving the single leaf-addition rule $L_1$ under quotient exploration (\texttt{ExploreFromCanonicalStatesOnly -> True}) for $k = 0 \dots 11$ steps ($V = 1 \dots 12$ joints) reproduces $12$ exact terms of \texttt{OEIS A000055} ($987$ cumulative unlabelled trees vs.\ $\sum k! = 43{,}954{,}714$ raw labelled trajectories reported in \texttt{StepStatistics}, a \textbf{$44{,}533.65\times$ state-count reduction} in $25\text{--}85\text{ ms}$) and spans all $35/35$ \texttt{LimbformDB} tree classes with $\le 12$ joints ($532/555 = 95.86\%$).
  \item \textbf{Null-Baseline Audit of Anterior--Posterior Crop Curves \& Lean~4 Arithmetic Lemmas:} Fitting a 2-parameter logistic to the 4 trunk-excision positions of Oviedo et al.\ (2010) Series S1 (1-octanol, with intact-head \texttt{tail\_crop} fixed at $0$) yields $r = 0.9823$ on S1 and $r = 0.9088$ ($\text{MAE} = 14.9\%$) on held-out innexin-RNAi Series S2, but is matched on S1 by a \textbf{0-parameter linear staircase null} ($r = 0.9829$) and beaten on S2 by a \textbf{0-parameter direct copy of S1} ($r = 0.9479$)---showing that crop-position curves alone do not uniquely identify multiway rule weights without a coupled biophysical solver. Four supporting arithmetic lemmas are kernel-checked in Lean~4 (\texttt{0 sorry}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: Graph Formalisms in Regenerative Biology}

When Daniel Lobo and Michael Levin published the \textbf{Planform}~\cite{Lobo2013Planform,Lobo2013BiologyOpen} and \textbf{Limbform}~\cite{Lobo2014Limbform,Lobo2014Regeneration} databases, they established the standard mathematical graph encoding for experimental regenerative biology:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PlanformDB~2.5.0}~\cite{Lobo2013Planform,Lobo2013BiologyOpen}: $369$ region-encoded planarian morphologies ($372$ \texttt{Morphology} rows, of which $3$ outcomes---\texttt{\#38 Lethal}, \texttt{\#333 Midbody Buldge}, and \texttt{\#359 Double head, side tail}---carry zero region records) across $1{,}181$ regions (\texttt{Head}, \texttt{Trunk}, \texttt{Tail}), border links, and internal organs (\texttt{Eye}, \texttt{Brain}, \texttt{Pharynx}, \texttt{VNC}).
  \item \textbf{LimbformDB~2.1.0}~\cite{Lobo2014Limbform,Lobo2014Regeneration}: $555$ segment-encoded limb morphologies across amphibians (axolotl, newt, \emph{Xenopus}), crustaceans, insects, and arachnids across $3{,}146$ joints, segment links, \texttt{LinkSubJoint} ectopic grafts, and digits.
\end{itemize}

In the original \texttt{Planform} software~\cite{Lobo2013Planform}, each morphology is stored as a full attributed graph and compared against other morphologies via heuristic \textbf{graph edit distance (GED)}, while each new geometric, organ, or naming variant in the literature is assigned a separate \texttt{Morphology} ID. This raises three concrete questions that can now be answered exactly using the standalone C++20 \texttt{hg\_evolve} engine from the Wolfram Physics Project (\texttt{WolframInstitute/HypergraphRewritingEngine}, \texttt{v1.0.0-rc2}; Assar 2026~\cite{Assar2026,Wolfram2020}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exact Isomorphism Census:} Up to exact McKay individualization--refinement (IR) graph isomorphism (\texttt{ir\_core.hpp}), how many distinct topological region graphs, unlabelled skeletons, and organ-annotated graphs exist across the $369$ \texttt{PlanformDB} rows and $555$ \texttt{LimbformDB} rows, and which classes are lost if morphologies are summarized only by region-count vectors $(\#\text{Head}, \#\text{Trunk}, \#\text{Tail}, \#\text{Links})$?
  \item \textbf{Recall vs.\ Precision of Generative DPO Grammars:} When we formulate blastema sprouting and fate specification as multiway double-pushout (DPO) hypergraph rewrite rules (\texttt{R1--R8} vs.\ the 3-rule primitive leaf-addition/recoloring basis $\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$), what is the trade-off between \emph{empirical recall} (fraction of the $38$ observed \texttt{PlanformDB} classes reached) and \emph{generative precision} (fraction of generated canonical graphs that correspond to empirically observed planarians)?
  \item \textbf{Quotient State-Space Reduction:} When enumerating multi-step branched structures up to $V = 12$ joints (\texttt{LimbformDB}), how effectively does McKay IR quotient exploration (\texttt{ExploreFromCanonicalStatesOnly}) collapse the $k!$ vertex-labelling multiplicity?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Hypergraph Encoding \& Exact McKay IR Isomorphism Census}

\subsection{Encoding Typed Anatomical Graphs as Ordered Hypergraphs}

In the Lobo--Levin formalism~\cite{Lobo2013BiologyOpen,Lobo2014Regeneration}, a planarian morphology $M = (V, E_{\text{link}}, E_{\text{organ}})$ is a finite graph whose vertices $v \in V$ carry a region type $\tau(v) \in \{\mathtt{Trunk}, \mathtt{Head}, \mathtt{Tail}\}$, connected by undirected anatomical adjacency links $\{u, v\} \in E_{\text{link}}$. Because \texttt{HypergraphRewritingEngine} operates on multisets of ordered hyperedges of arity $1 \le a \le 16$ (\texttt{SPEC.md} \S1) and preserves arity and within-edge vertex order under McKay IR canonicalization (\texttt{ir\_core.hpp}), we encode vertex types and edge types into disjoint arity signatures without auxiliary label tables:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathtt{Trunk}(v) &\;\mapsto\; [v] \quad (\text{arity } 1), &
    \mathtt{Head}(v) &\;\mapsto\; [v, v] \quad (\text{arity } 2 \text{ self-loop}), \\
    \mathtt{Tail}(v) &\;\mapsto\; [v, v, v] \quad (\text{arity } 3 \text{ self-loop}), &
    \mathtt{Link}\{u, v\} &\;\mapsto\; [u, v, u, v],\, [v, u, v, u] \quad (\text{symmetric arity } 4).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:encoding}
\end{equation}
When organ landmarks are included, each organ attached to region $v$ allocates a fresh leaf vertex $w$ with organ-specific arity $\mathtt{Eye} \mapsto 5$, $\mathtt{Brain} \mapsto 6$, $\mathtt{Pharynx} \mapsto 7$, and $\mathtt{VNC} \mapsto 8$. For \texttt{LimbformDB~2.1.0}, joints are encoded as arity-1 hyperedges $[j]$, segments as symmetric arity-4 link pairs (splitting host segments along any attached \texttt{LinkSubJoint} ectopic graft sequence $j_{\text{from}} \to j_{\text{sub},1} \to \dots \to j_{\text{to}}$ so ectopic branches are topologically connected to their host segment), and distal digits as arity-3 hyperedges $[j_{\text{to}}, d, d]$.

\section{Recall vs.\ Precision of Generative DPO Rewrite Grammars}

\subsection{Domain-Specific Sprouting Rules (\texttt{R1--R8}) from Excised Fragment Seeds}

To evaluate how multiway DPO rewriting spans the $38$ empirical \texttt{PlanformDB} classes, we initialize \texttt{hg\_evolve} with the three single-region excised fragment seeds $\mathcal{S}_0 = \{[[0]]\ (\mathtt{Trunk}),\; [[0,0]]\ (\mathtt{Head}),\; [[0,0,0]]\ (\mathtt{Tail})\}$ and test two rule sets (implemented in \texttt{models/2026-wolfram-planform-multiway-rewriting.py}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Terminal Blastema Sprouting, \texttt{R1--R6}):} \texttt{R1} ($\mathtt{Trunk} \to \mathtt{Trunk} + \mathtt{Head}$), \texttt{R2} ($\mathtt{Trunk} \to \mathtt{Trunk} + \mathtt{Tail}$), \texttt{R3} ($\mathtt{Trunk} \to \mathtt{Trunk} + \mathtt{Trunk}$), \texttt{R4} ($\mathtt{Head} \to \mathtt{Head} + \mathtt{Tail}$), \texttt{R5} ($\mathtt{Head} \to \mathtt{Head} + \mathtt{Head}$), and \texttt{R6} ($\mathtt{Tail} \to \mathtt{Tail} + \mathtt{Tail}$). At step $6$, Tier~1 generates $2{,}738$ canonical states ($169{,}440$ events), recovering \textbf{$33/38$ classes ($86.8\%$ recall, covering $361/369 = 97.83\%$ of all worms in \texttt{PlanformDB})} with a \textbf{generative precision of $33/2{,}738 = 1.21\%$}.
  \item \textbf{Tier 2 (+ Intercalary Sprouting, \texttt{R1--R8}):} Adding \texttt{R7} ($\mathtt{Head} \to \mathtt{Head} + \mathtt{Trunk}$) and \texttt{R8} ($\mathtt{Tail} \to \mathtt{Tail} + \mathtt{Trunk}$) allows an already-specified \texttt{Head} or \texttt{Tail} to bud a second \texttt{Trunk}, recovering the $4$ remaining connected intercalary path classes (\texttt{\#113}, \texttt{\#116}, \texttt{\#282}, \texttt{\#286}) at step $6$ ($6{,}787$ canonical states, \textbf{$37/38 = 97.4\%$ recall}, $368/369 = 99.73\%$ of worms, \textbf{precision $37/6{,}787 = 0.55\%$}). Including the 2-component bisected trunk seed $[[0], [1]]$ (Tier~3) recovers \texttt{\#394 Lysis, split worm} ($38/38$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Three-Rule Primitive Basis $\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$ as a Universal 3-Colored Forest Generator}

Consider the 3-rule primitive DPO basis:
\begin{align}
  L_1\ (\texttt{L1\_UniversalSprout}): &\quad [[0]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[0],\, [1],\, [0,1,0,1],\, [1,0,1,0]] \quad (\text{leaf addition on unary vertex}), \label{eq:L1}\\
  D_1\ (\texttt{D1\_DiffHead}): &\quad [[0]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[0, 0]] \quad (\text{recolor } \mathtt{Trunk} \to \mathtt{Head}), \label{eq:D1}\\
  D_2\ (\texttt{D2\_DiffTail}): &\quad [[0]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[0, 0, 0]] \quad (\text{recolor } \mathtt{Trunk} \to \mathtt{Tail}). \label{eq:D2}
\end{align}
Because $D_1$ and $D_2$ consume the unary hyperedge $[[0]]$, any derivation in $\mathcal{B}_3$ from an undifferentiated seed $[[0]]$ can be reordered into a **two-phase normal form** where all $L$ leaf-addition steps ($L_1$) fire first to build an unlabelled tree on $V = 1 + L$ vertices, followed by $H + Ta$ recoloring steps ($D_1, D_2$) at total step depth $k_{\text{prim}} = L + H + Ta$. (Note that unlike \texttt{R4--R8}, where an already-differentiated \texttt{Head} $[v,v]$ or \texttt{Tail} $[v,v,v]$ sprouts a new neighbor directly, $L_1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:L1} matches only $[v]$; thus $\mathcal{B}_3$ reaches the same target states from an undifferentiated seed by postponing $D_1, D_2$ after $L_1$, rather than by literal operator composition on differentiated seeds.)

Crucially, because every finite tree can be built by successive leaf additions ($L_1$) and any subset of vertices can then be colored \texttt{Head} ($D_1$) or \texttt{Tail} ($D_2$), \textbf{$\mathcal{B}_3$ is a universal generator of all 3-colored forests}. Measuring its precision and recall across depth $d$ (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}C) demonstrates why an unconstrained rewrite grammar is a \emph{syntactic substrate} rather than a biological predictor:
\begin{itemize}
  \item At depth $d = 6$, $\mathcal{B}_3$ generates $817$ canonical states, recovering $22/38$ \texttt{PlanformDB} classes (\textbf{precision $2.69\%$}).
  \item At depth $d = 10$, $\mathcal{B}_3$ generates $68{,}858$ canonical states, recovering $36/38$ classes (\textbf{precision $0.052\%$}).
  \item At depth $d = 12$, $\mathcal{B}_3$ generates $754{,}520$ canonical states, recovering $37/38$ classes (\textbf{precision $0.0049\%$}), while depth $d = 13$ (needed for the 7-tail star \texttt{\#382} at cost $L + Ta = 6 + 7 = 13$) exceeds host memory.
\end{itemize}
Thus, the empirical content of \texttt{PlanformDB} reachability under $\mathcal{B}_3$ is that \textbf{all $369$ recorded planarian morphologies are forests of $\le 7$ regions}; selecting the $38$ biological outcomes out of the $\sim 10^5\text{--}10^6$ syntactically reachable 3-colored trees is performed entirely by the continuous bioelectric and morphogen dynamics.

\section{Discussion: Relation to Lobo's Graph-Grammar and Target-Morphology Corpus (2010--2015)}

Connecting \texttt{HypergraphRewritingEngine} to \texttt{PlanformDB} and \texttt{LimbformDB} clarifies the relationship between the two halves of Daniel Lobo's research corpus:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Regulated Graph Grammars (M\'alaga, 2010--2012):} In \texttt{ELSA}/\texttt{ELSA3}~\cite{Lobo2010Biosystems,Lobo2011TCS,Lobo2012BookChapter}, Lobo, Vico, and Dassow showed that edge-replacement graph grammars work well only when paired with a \emph{regulatory controller} (a string domain or an intracellular Kauffman Boolean network with a differentiation node) that restricts which rules fire where.
  \item \textbf{Target-Morphology Databases \& The Inverse Problem (Tufts, 2013--2015):} In Lobo et al.\ (2014, \emph{J.\ R.\ Soc.\ Interface})~\cite{Lobo2014LinearEncoding} and Lobo \& Levin (2015, \emph{PLOS Comput.\ Biol.})~\cite{Lobo2015PLOSCompBiol}, the authors noted that an unregulated recursive grammar cannot solve the inverse problem of regeneration without a regulatory or target-encoding layer. Our precision/recall audit in Section~3 quantifies that exact distinction on \texttt{PlanformDB}: the unregulated 3-rule DPO basis $\mathcal{B}_3 = \{L_1, D_1, D_2\}$ defines the complete syntactic space of 3-colored forests, but because its precision at depth $12$ is $0.0049\%$ ($37/754{,}520$), all biological specificity resides in the regulatory dynamics (\texttt{ELSA}'s Boolean controller or the bioelectric/PDE network inferred in~\cite{Lobo2015PLOSCompBiol,Cervera2021}), while McKay IR canonicalization (\texttt{ir\_core.hpp}) provides exact 64-bit isomorphism hashing across the empirical catalog.
\end{enumerate}
