\begin{abstract}
For over a century, D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson's coordinate transformation grids (\emph{On Growth and Form}, 1917, Ch.\ XVII, pp.\ 748--751, Figs.\ 373--382) have stood as the founding visual metaphor of mathematical biology. Since Fred Bookstein's introduction of thin-plate splines (TPS, 1989) and the standardization of sparse landmark protocols in ichthyology (Claverie \& Wainwright 2014; Friedman et al.\ 2019), evolutionary biologists have routinely equated fitting transformations to 17 homologous lateral landmarks with Thompson's theory of transformations. Yet in two recent critiques, Bookstein (2023, 2024) argued that exact TPS interpolants overfit biological form and should be replaced by fitted low-dimensional parametric coordinate grids that retain explicit residuals.

Here we perform a comprehensive mathematical and empirical evaluation of all four of Thompson's 1917 fish coordinate families --- \textbf{I.\ Oblique shear} (5 parameters, Figs.\ 373--374), \textbf{II.\ Coaxial circles} (8 parameters, Figs.\ 375--376), \textbf{III.\ Fan of coordinates} (8 parameters, Figs.\ 377--380), and \textbf{IV.\ Hyperbolic/radial expansion} (8 parameters, Figs.\ 381--382) --- alongside Procrustes similarity (4p), the uniform affine component (6p), quadratic polynomial trend surfaces (12p), and exact TPS (34p) across: (i) all six exact species pairs Thompson drew in 1917, digitized both as 17 homologous landmarks and as 600-semilandmark whole-body outlines, and (ii) 791 Indo-Pacific shore-fish species (4,505 taxonomic pairs and 4,334 time-calibrated divergences on the Rabosky et al.\ 2018 megaphylogeny).

We report four primary discoveries:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The 17-Landmark Skeletal Wireframe vs.\ 600-Semilandmark Outline Discrepancy:} In standard ichthyological landmark schemes (Claverie \& Wainwright 2014), 9 of the 17 landmarks ($53\%$ of the ordinary least-squares weight) lie in the craniofacial partition ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}2, 11\text{--}17$), while the remaining 8 mark only where the fin bases insert into the body wall ($\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{--}10$) --- omitting the entire distal contour of the dorsal fin, anal fin, caudal fin, and ventral body profile. Because craniofacial proportions and median-fin insertions evolve modularly across families (fin-insertion Procrustes RMS residuals are $1.48\times$ to $2.03\times$ larger than craniofacial residuals), fitting a coordinate grid to the 17 landmarks on interfamily pairs such as \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} or \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia} \textbf{actively degrades the real 600-semilandmark fish silhouette compared to no non-rigid deformation at all}: the symmetric non-overlapping area $|A \Delta B|$ worsens from $40.6\%$ to $56.2\%$ of target fish area on \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} ($\IoU: 0.662 \to 0.618$) and from $102.2\%$ to $143.7\%$ on \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia} ($\IoU: 0.417 \to 0.389$).
  \item \textbf{Direct 600-Semilandmark Outline Fitting Vindicates Thompson's 1917 Transformations:} When the exact same 5- to 8-parameter Thompson coordinate families are fitted \emph{directly} to the 600-semilandmark whole-body outlines (anchored at Bookstein's two axial baseline endpoints, snout tip and caudal peduncle midpoint) rather than the 17 skeletal landmarks, Thompson's transformations are recovered across every 1917 pair with $100\%$ diffeomorphic bijectivity ($\det D\Phi > 0$): non-overlapping area $|A \Delta B|$ is cut in half on \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} ($56.2\% \to \mathbf{28.5\%}$, $\IoU = \mathbf{0.749}$), cut $2.5\times$ on \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia} ($143.7\% \to \mathbf{57.6\%}$, $\IoU = \mathbf{0.599}$), reduced to $\mathbf{17.2\%}$ on \emph{Polyprion} $\to$ \emph{Pristigenys} ($\IoU = \mathbf{0.839}$), reduced $3.4\times$ on \emph{Pristigenys} $\to$ \emph{Scorpaena} ($120.1\% \to \mathbf{35.5\%}$), reduced to $\mathbf{45.6\%}$ on \emph{Argyropelecus} $\to$ \emph{Sternoptyx}, and reduced to $\mathbf{49.0\%}$ on \emph{Diodon} $\to$ \emph{Mola mola} ($\IoU = \mathbf{0.592}$).
  \item \textbf{A $\sim 45$--$50\text{ Myr}$ Macroevolutionary Disparity Transition and $\Delta\AICc$ Parsimony Optimum Across 791 Species:} Across 4,334 dated pairs on the Rabosky et al.\ (2018) tree, 8-parameter Thompson grids pull median 17-landmark Procrustes RMS residuals down inside the intrageneric disparity band ($\sigma_{\text{genus}} \le 0.0110$), reducing pair residuals below $\sigma_{\text{genus}}(T)$ in $\mathbf{53.0\%}$ of pairs diverged $<10\text{ Myr}$, $\mathbf{61.3\%}$ at $10$--$25\text{ Myr}$, and $\mathbf{49.7\%}$ at $25$--$50\text{ Myr}$, before transitioning across $\sim 45$--$50\text{ Myr}$ ($t^* = 45.8\text{ Myr}$ on $n=187$ phylogenetically independent node-disjoint pairs) to $\mathbf{10.6\%}$ ($50$--$80\text{ Myr}$) and $\mathbf{0.0\%}$ ($>110\text{ Myr}$) as median-fin insertions decouple from the cranium (Satterfield, Claverie \& Wainwright 2024). By small-sample corrected $\AICc$ ($n = 34$), 8-parameter Thompson laws beat the 6-parameter uniform affine component in $\mathbf{82.2\%}$ of intrafamily pairs and beat 12-parameter quadratic trend surfaces in $\mathbf{84.9\%}$ of intrafamily and $\mathbf{92.1\%}$ of interfamily pairs.
  \item \textbf{Superiority Over Standard 600-Semilandmark Curve TPS/EFD \& Phylomorphospace Convergence:} Holding the 600-semilandmark outline input fixed across all six 1917 pairs, standard 600-semilandmark curve TPS (Bookstein 1997) and 12p quadratic surfaces fold the 2D interior coordinate grid ($\det D\Phi \le 0$ on $6/6$ and $4/6$ pairs) and degrade out-of-sample prediction of held-out interior skeletal landmarks by up to $2.4\times$, whereas 8p Thompson grids remain $100\%$ diffeomorphic and accurately predict held-out internal anatomy from the outer outline alone. Across all 791 species (Leave-One-Family-Out CV across 48 families), Thompson's 1917 equations capture $\mathbf{92.0\%}$ (median) / $\mathbf{95.6\%}$ (mean) of the optimal 8-parameter empirical Relative-Warp variance (Rohlf 1993) and $\mathbf{88.6\%}$ of the primary $(\mathrm{PC1}, \mathrm{PC2})$ phylomorphospace plane (Sidlauskas 2008), while Phenotypic Trajectory Analysis (Adams \& Collyer 2009) shows that distantly related clades ($>95\text{ Myr}$) converging on deep-bodied or elongate/fusiform body plans follow strongly parallel 8p Thompson trajectories ($r_{\mathrm{vec}} = +0.693$ and $+0.714$, vs.\ $-0.699$ between divergent clades) alongside divergent ``many-to-one'' craniofacial and fin-insertion residuals (Wainwright et al.\ 2005).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson, \emph{On Growth and Form}, transformation grids, geometric morphometrics, thin-plate splines, semilandmarks, Relative Warps, phylomorphospace, phenotypic trajectory analysis, morphological integration and modularity, many-to-one mapping.\\
\textbf{Target Venues:} \emph{Proceedings of the Royal Society B} $\cdot$ \emph{Evolution} $\cdot$ \emph{Systematic Biology} $\cdot$ \emph{Evolutionary Biology}.

\section{Introduction: A 109-Year Methodological Gap}

In Chapter XVII of \emph{On Growth and Form} (1917, pp.\ 748--751, Figs.\ 373--382), D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson presented ten drawings spanning six pairwise transformations of teleost fishes~\cite{Thompson1917}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Family I (Oblique Shear, Figs.\ 373--374, p.\ 748):} The deep-sea hatchetfish \emph{Argyropelecus olfersii} deformed into \emph{Sternoptyx diaphana} by inclining rectangular coordinates at $\approx 70^\circ$.
  \item \textbf{Family II (Coaxial Circles, Figs.\ 375--376, p.\ 749):} A parrotfish (\emph{Scarus} sp.) deformed into an angelfish (\emph{Pomacanthus}) via a system of approximately coaxial circles.
  \item \textbf{Family III (Fan of Coordinates, Figs.\ 377--380, p.\ 750):} A four-species sequence linking the wreckfish \emph{Polyprion americanus}, the bigeye \emph{Pseudopriacanthus altus} ($=$ \emph{Pristigenys alta}), the scorpionfish \emph{Scorpaena scrofa}, and the rhomboid boarfish \emph{Antigonia capros} via diverging and converging fan lines.
  \item \textbf{Family IV (Hyperbolic / Radial Expansion, Figs.\ 381--382, p.\ 751):} The porcupinefish \emph{Diodon} deformed into the ocean sunfish \emph{Orthagoriscus mola} ($=$ \emph{Mola mola}) by warping vertical lines into concentric arcs and horizontals into hyperbolae that flare exponentially toward the caudal margin.
\end{enumerate}

Thompson drew these grids by hand over continuous lateral outlines of whole fishes (including their dorsal, anal, and caudal fins) and gave no algebraic estimation procedure~\cite{Bookstein1978}. When geometric morphometrics was formalized in the 1980s and 1990s, two methodological substitutions occurred simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{From Low-Dimensional Parametric Coordinate Laws to Exact 34-Parameter Thin-Plate Splines:} Bookstein (1989) introduced the thin-plate spline (TPS)~\cite{Bookstein1989}, which interpolates $p$ landmarks in 2D using $2p$ parameters with zero landmark residual. As Bookstein recently emphasized~\cite{Bookstein2023,Bookstein2024}, an exact interpolant cannot falsify Thompson's hypothesis because it fits \emph{any} pair of landmark configurations with zero error, absorbing all local modular rearrangements into localized bending energy.
  \item \textbf{From Continuous Whole-Body Outlines to 17 Craniofacial-and-Insertion Landmarks:} Because museum specimens and underwater photographs vary in fin posture (erect vs.\ depressed dorsal and anal spines), comparative ichthyologists standardized a 17-landmark protocol (Claverie \& Wainwright 2014; Friedman et al.\ 2019)~\cite{ClaverieWainwright2014,Friedman2019} in which \textbf{9 of the 17 landmarks ($53\%$) lie inside the craniofacial partition} ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}2, 11\text{--}17$) and \textbf{8 mark only the basal insertion points of the fins on the body wall} ($\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{--}10$), leaving \emph{zero landmarks} on the distal contour of the dorsal fin, anal fin, caudal fin, or ventral body profile.
\end{enumerate}

In this study, we test what happens when Thompson's four low-dimensional coordinate families are formalized algebraically and evaluated both on the standard 17-landmark dataset across 791 species and on the actual 600-semilandmark whole-body outlines.

\section{Mathematical Formulation of Thompson's Four Coordinate Families}

Let $S \in \R^{N \times 2}$ and $T \in \R^{N \times 2}$ denote source and target configurations in the Generalized Procrustes Analysis (GPA) consensus frame (unit centroid size, aligned horizontal snout-to-peduncle axis). Between the 4-parameter similarity baseline ($d_0 = \mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{OPA}(S, T), T)$) and the 12-parameter quadratic polynomial trend surface~\cite{Bookstein1978,Bookstein2024}, we formalize Thompson's four 1917 transformation families as nested parametric maps:

\begin{definition}[Thompson's Four Parametric Coordinate Families]
Let $(u, v) = (x, y, 1) M$ denote the standard 6-parameter uniform (affine) component with $M \in \R^{3 \times 2}$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Family I --- Oblique Shear (5 parameters; Figs.\ 373--374):} In the horizontal body-axis frame, vertical dorsoventral coordinates shear obliquely along the body axis while scaling dorsoventrally without rotating the horizontal axis:
  \begin{equation}
    x' = a x + g y + t_x, \qquad y' = d y + t_y, \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{incl}} = \arctan(d / g).
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Family II --- Oblique Coaxial Circles (8 parameters; Figs.\ 375--376):} Composition of a uniform affine map $(u, v)$ with a complex M\"obius inversion $z = u + i v \mapsto w = z / (c z + 1)$ for $c = c_1 + i c_2 \in \C$, which maps Cartesian grid lines into two pencils of coaxial circles in the $(x', y')$ plane intersecting at $w(0) = (0, 0)$ and $w(\infty) = 1/c = (c_1/|c|^2,\, -c_2/|c|^2)$ (with pre-image pole at $z = -1/c = (-c_1/|c|^2,\, c_2/|c|^2)$):
  \begin{equation}
    x' + i y' = \frac{u + i v}{(c_1 + i c_2)(u + i v) + 1}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Family III --- Bilinear Fan of Coordinates (8 parameters; Figs.\ 377--380):} Composition of a uniform affine map $(u, v)$ with a bilinear diverging/converging fan $(f_1, f_2) \in \R^2$ in which horizontal lines fan linearly with $u$ and vertical lines fan linearly with $v$:
  \begin{equation}
    x' = u\,(1 + f_1 v), \qquad y' = v\,(1 + f_2 u).
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Family IV --- Hyperbolic / Radial Expansion (8 parameters; Figs.\ 381--382):} Composition of a uniform affine map $(u, v)$ with parabolic vertical bowing $r_1 v^2$ and exponential caudally directed dorsoventral flare $\exp(r_2 u)$:
  \begin{equation}
    x' = u + r_1 v^2, \qquad y' = v\,\exp(r_2 u).
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For landmark fits ($N = 17$, $n_{\mathrm{obs}} = 34$ scalar coordinates), parameters are estimated via multi-start Levenberg--Marquardt optimization initialized at the exact ordinary least-squares uniform affine solution $M$ with $(p_7, p_8) \in \{(0,0), (\pm 0.4, 0), (0, \pm 0.4)\}$. Synthetic self-tests confirm that all three 8-parameter nonlinear solvers recover ground-truth parameters to machine precision ($\mathrm{RMS} < 10^{-8}$). Small-sample corrected Akaike Information Criterion ($\AICc$) for a $k$-parameter model on $n = 34$ scalar landmark coordinates is computed as
\begin{equation}
  \AICc(k) = n \ln\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n}\right) + 2k + \frac{2k(k+1)}{n - k - 1}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n} = \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{RMS}^2.
\end{equation}

\section{Direct 600-Semilandmark Outline Fitting \& Non-Overlapping Silhouette Area}

To test Thompson's transformations on actual whole-body fish outlines rather than 8-vertex trunk wireframes, we segmented continuous $N_B = 600$-semilandmark closed boundary contours $B_S, B_T \subset \R^2$ for Thompson's 1917 species from archival museum plates and field photographs, registered to the Procrustes frame. To prevent non-homologous tangential sliding around the perimeter --- the classic failure mode of unanchored outline morphometrics critiqued by Bookstein~\cite{Bookstein1978,Bookstein1997,Bookstein2023} --- our direct 600-semilandmark outline estimator minimizes the symmetric bidirectional Chamfer contour distance anchored at Bookstein's two-point axial baseline ($\mathrm{LM}\,2$, anterior snout tip $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{snout}}$, and $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ped}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{LM}\,5 + \mathrm{LM}\,6)$, caudal peduncle midpoint), initialized at the 17-landmark solution:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sil}}(\theta) &= \frac{1}{2|B_S|}\sum_{\mathbf{p} \in \Phi_\theta(B_S)} \min_{\mathbf{q} \in B_T} \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q}\|^2 + \frac{1}{2|B_T|}\sum_{\mathbf{q} \in B_T} \min_{\mathbf{p} \in \Phi_\theta(B_S)} \|\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{p}\|^2 \nonumber\\
  &\quad + \lambda_{\mathrm{ax}}\!\left(\|\Phi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{snout}}^S) - \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{snout}}^T\|^2 + \|\Phi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ped}}^S) - \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ped}}^T\|^2\right),
\end{align}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{ax}} = 0.15$. Thus zero internal craniofacial landmarks ($\mathrm{LM}\,11\text{--}17$) and zero dorsal/anal fin-base insertions ($\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{--}4, 7\text{--}8$) enter the contour fit; the two baseline endpoints solely lock the anterior--posterior body axis so the head cannot rotate into the tail. Given a warped source silhouette $A = \Phi_\theta(B_S)$ and target silhouette $B = B_T$, let $\Omega_A, \Omega_B \subset \R^2$ denote their filled interior regions (rasterized on a $600 \times 600$ grid). We decompose the non-overlapping area into two physically interpretable components:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|}_{\text{Non-Overlapping Area}} = \underbrace{|\Omega_B \setminus \Omega_A|}_{\substack{\text{Missing Target Area (Red)}\\ \text{(unreached fins / keels)}}} + \underbrace{|\Omega_A \setminus \Omega_B|}_{\substack{\text{Excess Source Overhang (Blue)}\\ \text{(source spilling outside target)}}}, \quad \IoU = \frac{|\Omega_A \cap \Omega_B|}{|\Omega_A \cup \Omega_B|}.
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:area_diff} and Figure~\ref{fig:area_diff} expose the first core finding of this paper:
\begin{finding}[Why 17-Landmark Fitting Degrades Whole-Body Fish Silhouettes]
In three of Thompson's transformations (\emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus}, \emph{Argyropelecus} $\to$ \emph{Sternoptyx}, and \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia}), fitting the coordinate grid to the 17 homologous landmarks \textbf{increases the total non-overlapping area $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$ above the unwarped baseline} (from $40.6\% \to 56.2\%$, $58.4\% \to 63.0\%$, and $102.2\% \to 143.7\%$, respectively). Even an exact 34-parameter Thin-Plate Spline fitted to the 17 landmarks of \emph{Scarus ghobban} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus semicirculatus} reduces silhouette $\IoU$ from $0.662$ (unwarped) to $0.629$. This occurs because 9 of the 17 landmarks ($53\%$ of the least-squares weight) lie in the craniofacial partition while $0\%$ lie on the distal fin margins; when craniofacial proportions and median-fin profiles decouple across families, optimizing the 17 landmarks steers the coordinate grid in the wrong direction for the posterior body and fins. Conversely, fitting Thompson's exact same 5- to 8-parameter coordinate families directly to the 600-semilandmark outlines cuts $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$ to $\mathbf{28.5\%}$ on \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} ($\IoU = 0.749$) and $\mathbf{17.2\%}$ on \emph{Polyprion} $\to$ \emph{Pristigenys} ($\IoU = 0.839$).
\end{finding}

\section{Macroevolutionary Scaling Across 791 Species \& 4,334 Dated Divergences}

While the 17-landmark dataset omits distal fin contours, it provides an exceptional measure of the \emph{craniofacial-and-fin-insertion skeleton} across 791 Indo-Pacific shore-fish species (260 genera, 48 families; Claverie \& Wainwright 2014; Friedman et al.\ 2019). Linking these 791 species to the Rabosky et al.\ (2018) time-calibrated Actinopterygian megaphylogeny~\cite{Rabosky2018} yields 4,334 pairs with exact divergence times (Myr). For each pair $(S \to T)$, we evaluate both the continuous post-warp Procrustes RMS residual and the proportion of pairs whose post-warp residual falls within the target genus's own intrageneric Procrustes disparity ($\sigma_{\mathrm{genus}}(T)$, median $0.0110$ Procrustes RMS across multi-species genera).

\begin{figure}[H]

\section{Methodological Stress-Tests: Partition Ablation, Diffeomorphic Bijectivity, Bidirectional Symmetry, and Phylogenetically Independent Contrasts}

To ensure that our findings cannot be attributed to landmark partition selection, non-invertible grid folding, directional asymmetry ($S \to T$ vs.\ $T \to S$), or pairwise phylogenetic pseudoreplication, we performed four quantitative stress-tests (\texttt{thompson/run\_rigor\_stress\_tests.py}; Figure~\ref{fig:rigor} and Table~\ref{tab:rigor}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Double Partition Ablation (9-Craniofacial-LM vs.\ All 17-LM vs.\ 10-Outer-Trunk-LM vs.\ 600-Semilandmark Outline):}
  A natural question is whether the failure of 17-landmark fitting on whole-fish silhouettes is caused \emph{solely} by the 7 internal skull/eye/opercle landmarks ($\mathrm{LM}\,11\text{--}17$), or whether removing them and fitting only the 10 outer body-wall landmarks ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}10$: snout tip, jaw tip, dorsal fin base, caudal base, anal fin base, and pectoral base) suffices to recover the silhouette. Table~\ref{tab:rigor} and Figure~\ref{fig:rigor}A demonstrate that \textbf{both limitations of the 17-landmark scheme are independently fatal}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Limitation 1 (Craniofacial Landmark Density \& Extrapolation Bias):} Fitting the 9 craniofacial landmarks alone ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}2, 11\text{--}17$) on \emph{Scarus ghobban} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus semicirculatus} extrapolates across the posterior $75\%$ of the body, inflating non-overlapping silhouette area $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$ to $\mathbf{587.1\%}$ of target fish area (and $\mathbf{8{,}925\%}$ on \emph{Diodon} $\to$ \emph{Mola mola}).
    \item \emph{Limitation 2 (Distal Median-Fin \& Ventral-Keel Omission):} Even when all 7 internal skull/opercle landmarks ($\mathrm{LM}\,11\text{--}17$) are removed and Thompson's grids are fitted \emph{exclusively} to the 10 outer trunk-insertion landmarks ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}10$), the silhouette non-overlapping area remains worse than the unwarped baseline on \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} ($\mathbf{54.5\%}$ vs.\ $40.6\%$ unwarped), \emph{Argyropelecus} $\to$ \emph{Sternoptyx} ($\mathbf{64.0\%}$ vs.\ $58.4\%$ unwarped), \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia} ($\mathbf{153.6\%}$ vs.\ $102.2\%$ unwarped), and \emph{Diodon} $\to$ \emph{Mola mola} ($\mathbf{77.1\%}$ vs.\ $70.8\%$ unwarped) because $\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{--}10$ sit at the basal body-wall insertions and omit $100\%$ of the distal dorsal fin, anal fin, caudal fin, and ventral keel. Only direct 600-semilandmark outline fitting resolves both limitations simultaneously ($|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B| = \mathbf{28.5\%}, \mathbf{45.6\%}, \mathbf{57.6\%},$ and $\mathbf{49.0\%}$, respectively).
  \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Diffeomorphic Bijectivity \& Positive-Definite Jacobian Verification ($\det D\Phi(x,y) > 0$):}
  Computing the exact $2 \times 2$ Jacobian matrix $D\Phi_\theta(x,y)$ across every interior and boundary point of the source fish silhouette confirms that \textbf{100.0\% of all points across all six 600-semilandmark outline transformations have strictly positive Jacobian determinant} ($\min_{(x,y) \in \Omega_S} \det D\Phi(x,y) \in [0.374, 1.150] > 0$; Table~\ref{tab:rigor} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rigor}B), with bounded principal strain anisotropy ($\mathrm{median}\;\sigma_1/\sigma_2 \in [1.32\times, 4.40\times]$) and zero topological grid folding anywhere.

  \item \textbf{Bidirectional Symmetry ($S \to T$ vs.\ $T \to S$) \& Spatial Sector Cross-Validation:}
  Fitting Thompson's coordinate families in the reverse direction (Target $\to$ Source) on the 600-semilandmark outlines confirms bidirectional recovery across all six pairs (Table~\ref{tab:rigor} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rigor}C): \emph{Pomacanthus} $\to$ \emph{Scarus} achieves $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B| = \mathbf{26.0\%}$ ($\IoU = \mathbf{0.765}$ vs.\ $40.8\%$ unwarped), \emph{Antigonia} $\to$ \emph{Scorpaena} achieves $\mathbf{39.3\%}$ ($\IoU = \mathbf{0.622}$ vs.\ $71.0\%$ unwarped), \emph{Pristigenys} $\to$ \emph{Polyprion} achieves $\mathbf{18.1\%}$ ($\IoU = \mathbf{0.834}$), and \emph{Mola} $\to$ \emph{Diodon} achieves $\mathbf{32.3\%}$ ($\IoU = \mathbf{0.712}$ vs.\ $139.3\%$ unwarped). Furthermore, holding out contiguous $100$-point ($60^\circ$) boundary sectors via 6-fold spatial block cross-validation reduces out-of-sample contour distance by $13.3\%\text{--}24.2\%$ on smooth perciform profiles (\emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus}, \emph{Polyprion} $\to$ \emph{Pristigenys}, \emph{Pristigenys} $\to$ \emph{Scorpaena}), while identifying the acute dorsal blade and ventral keel apices of \emph{Sternoptyx} and \emph{Antigonia} as localized high-leverage structural boundaries.

  \item \textbf{Unbinned Continuous Logistic Transition \& Node-Disjoint Independent Pairs Control:}
  Fitting an unbinned continuous logistic decay law $P(t) = L / [1 + \exp(k(t - t^*))]$ across all $4{,}334$ dated pairs yields a low-age plateau of $L = 57.6\%$ and an all-pair half-saturation midpoint of $t^* = 58.2\text{ Myr}$ ($95\%$ bootstrap CI: $[53.0, 63.6]\text{ Myr}$, with the binned transition onset occurring across the $45\text{--}50\text{ Myr}$ boundary; Fig.~\ref{fig:rigor}D). Restricting the dataset to \textbf{strictly node-disjoint phylogenetically independent pairs} ($n = 187$, Felsenstein 1985~\cite{Felsenstein1985}, in which no species or genus is ever reused across pairs, eliminating $100\%$ of pairwise phylogenetic pseudoreplication) yields a node-disjoint inflection point of $\mathbf{t^* = 45.8\text{ Myr}}$, with $\mathbf{54.0\%}$ within $\sigma_{\mathrm{genus}}$ at $< 50\text{ Myr}$ versus $\mathbf{0.0\%}$ at $\ge 50\text{ Myr}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Benchmarking Against Competing 600-Semilandmark Outline Methods \& Phylomorphospace Convergence}

Two fundamental methodological questions arise when evaluating 600-semilandmark outlines and macroevolutionary morphospaces:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{When holding the 600-semilandmark outline input fixed, how do Thompson's 8-parameter 2D coordinate laws compare against standard 600-point outline methods such as Elliptic Fourier Descriptors (Kuhl \& Giardina 1982)~\cite{KuhlGiardina1982} or curve-semilandmark Thin-Plate Splines (Bookstein 1997)~\cite{Bookstein1997}?}
  \item \emph{Across all 791 species, how much of the empirical non-affine Relative-Warp variance (Rohlf 1993)~\cite{Rohlf1993} and phylomorphospace structure (Sidlauskas 2008)~\cite{Sidlauskas2008} is captured by Thompson's 1917 equations, and how do they separate convergent evolution from divergent evolution under Phenotypic Trajectory Analysis (Adams \& Collyer 2009; Stayton 2015)~\cite{AdamsCollyer2009,Stayton2015}?}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Head-to-Head Benchmark of Five 600-Semilandmark Outline Methods}

Table~\ref{tab:600pt_methods} and Figure~\ref{fig:wainwright_600pt}A--C evaluate five competing methods fitted to the \emph{exact same} 600-semilandmark outlines across all six of Thompson's 1917 pairs on four orthogonal criteria: (i) non-overlapping area $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$, (ii) Chamfer contour residual, (iii) global 2D diffeomorphic bijectivity ($\det D\Phi(x,y) > 0$ everywhere), and (iv) \textbf{out-of-sample (cross-validated) prediction of the 14 held-out interior skeletal landmarks} (eye, jaw, opercle, pectoral and median fin bases) when the coordinate map is fitted \emph{solely} to the 600-semilandmark outer outline:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Elliptic Fourier Descriptors (EFD, $k = 8$ params / 2 harmonics; Kuhl \& Giardina 1982):} Parameterizes a 1D closed boundary curve $(x(s), y(s))$ and defines \textbf{no 2D interior coordinate transformation $\Phi: \R^2 \to \R^2$} ($0\%$ ability to predict where internal craniofacial or axial landmarks move inside the body).
  \item \textbf{Standard 600-Semilandmark Curve Thin-Plate Spline (Bookstein 1997; Felice et al.\ 2018):} Because curve-semilandmark TPS pairs boundary points by perimeter arc-length ($s \in [0, 1]$; cf.~\cite{Bookstein1997,Felice2018SysBio}), disproportionate hypertrophy of the dorsal and anal fin lobes in \emph{Pomacanthus}, \emph{Sternoptyx}, \emph{Scorpaena}, \emph{Antigonia}, and \emph{Mola} slides semilandmarks non-homologously along the contour, \textbf{folding and inverting the 2D interior coordinate grid on $6$ of $6$ pairs ($0\%$ globally diffeomorphic; $\min \det D\Phi = -6.76$, with up to $25.1\%$ of the body domain inverted; Fig.~\ref{fig:wainwright_600pt}A)} and degrading \textbf{out-of-sample prediction of held-out interior skeletal landmarks on $6$ of $6$ pairs} (up to $2.4\times$ higher Procrustes RMS: $0.072$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.032}$ on \emph{Polyprion} $\to$ \emph{Pristigenys}; $0.107$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.045}$ on \emph{Pristigenys} $\to$ \emph{Scorpaena}; $0.134$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.091}$ on \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia}).
  \item \textbf{12-Parameter Quadratic Polynomial Trend Surface Fitted to the 600 Semilandmarks:} Folds the 2D coordinate grid ($\det D\Phi \le 0$) on \textbf{$4$ of $6$ pairs} ($63.4\%\text{--}95.4\%$ bijective, $\min \det D\Phi = -1.35$) and degrades held-out interior landmark prediction by up to $2.1\times$ ($0.092$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.045}$ on \emph{Pristigenys} $\to$ \emph{Scorpaena}; $0.148$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.091}$ on \emph{Scorpaena} $\to$ \emph{Antigonia}).
  \item \textbf{6-Parameter Uniform (Affine) Component vs.\ 8-Parameter Thompson Coordinate Grid on the 600 Semilandmarks:} Adding Thompson's 2 non-affine bending parameters to the 6p uniform component reduces Chamfer contour loss by $16.2\%\text{--}32.2\%$ on the exact same 600 points (reducing $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$ from $27.4\% \to \mathbf{22.6\%}$, $\IoU = 0.794$ on \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} under multi-start optimization) while maintaining $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ diffeomorphic bijectivity ($\det D\Phi > 0$ on $6/6$ pairs) and the lowest out-of-sample interior landmark error.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Empirical Relative Warps (Rohlf 1993), Phylomorphospace (Sidlauskas 2008), and Phenotypic Trajectory Analysis (Adams \& Collyer 2009)}

To test whether Thompson's 1917 coordinate families align with data-driven axes of teleost diversification across all 791 species, we decomposed total Procrustes variation from the consensus configuration $M_0$ into the 6-parameter uniform (affine) component plus the non-affine component, whose principal eigenvectors are \textbf{Relative Warps} (Rohlf 1993~\cite{Rohlf1993}; Fig.~\ref{fig:wainwright_600pt}D--F):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Leave-One-Family-Out Cross-Validation (LOFO-CV) Against Empirical 8p Relative Warps ($92.0\%$ of Theoretical Ceiling):}
  Across all 48 families ($N = 791$ species), the 6-parameter uniform (affine) component explains $\mathbf{25.1\%}$ median ($29.3\%$ mean) of shape variance from $M_0$, whereas the optimal data-driven 8-parameter Relative-Warp basis (6p uniform + empirical $\mathrm{RW1} + \mathrm{RW2}$ learned on the 47 training families) explains $\mathbf{55.3\%}$ median ($53.1\%$ mean). Without a single data-trained eigenvector, \textbf{Thompson's 1917 analytical 8p coordinate laws explain $\mathbf{50.9\%}$ median ($50.8\%$ mean) --- capturing $\mathbf{92.0\%}$ (median) / $\mathbf{95.6\%}$ (mean) of the theoretical 8-parameter Relative-Warp variance ceiling}.
  \item \textbf{Capturing the Primary Phylomorphospace Plane ($\mathrm{PC1} + \mathrm{PC2}$; Sidlauskas 2008):}
  Across the 62 multi-species reef-fish genera, the 6p uniform component captures only $33.1\%$ of Phylomorphospace $\mathrm{PC1}$ ($34.6\%$ of clade variance) and $48.9\%$ of the $(\mathrm{PC1}, \mathrm{PC2})$ plane, whereas \textbf{8-parameter Thompson coordinate grids capture $\mathbf{83.4\%}$ of $\mathrm{PC1}$, $\mathbf{95.8\%}$ of $\mathrm{PC2}$, and $\mathbf{88.6\%}$ of the primary $(\mathrm{PC1}, \mathrm{PC2})$ phylomorphospace plane}.
  \item \textbf{Phenotypic Trajectory Analysis (Adams \& Collyer 2009) \& Many-to-One Mapping (Wainwright et al.\ 2005):}
  Decomposing each genus's phenotypic trajectory from $M_0$ into its global 8p Thompson component plus its local craniofacial/fin-insertion residual ($\Delta Y_g = \Delta Y_g^{\mathrm{Thompson}} + \Delta Y_g^{\mathrm{Modular}}$) across 5 distantly related deep-bodied reef-fish families ($>95\text{ Myr}$ apart: Chaetodontidae, Pomacanthidae, Acanthuridae, Pomacentridae, Siganidae; 38 inter-family pairs) and 6 elongate/fusiform families (14 inter-family pairs) reveals that convergent clades follow \textbf{strongly parallel 8p Thompson trajectories} (median trajectory correlation $r_{\mathrm{vec}} = \cos \theta = \mathbf{+0.693}$ in deep-bodied clades and $\mathbf{+0.714}$ in elongate/fusiform clades, vs.\ $\mathbf{-0.699}$ between divergent clades, $p < 10^{-8}$), adding $+16.3\%$ to $+43.6\%$ explained variance over the 6p uniform component (\emph{Siganus} $4.2\% \to 41.6\%$, \emph{Acanthurus} $19.0\% \to 62.6\%$, \emph{Ctenochaetus} $37.2\% \to 68.7\%$). Meanwhile, their local craniofacial and fin-insertion residuals are nearly twice as divergent ($r_{\mathrm{vec}} = +0.416$), providing a direct geometric demonstration of \textbf{Wainwright et al.'s (2005) ``many-to-one mapping'' of form to function}~\cite{Wainwright2005}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Separating Generative Morphospace (Schluter's 1996 ``Lines of Least Resistance'') From Sympatric Trophic Competition}

A classic question in evolutionary quantitative genetics (Lande 1979; Schluter 1996~\cite{Schluter1996}) asks whether macroevolutionary clade diversification ($\mathbf{R}$, the between-genus covariance matrix across $\sim 150\text{ Myr}$) is channeled along the principal axis of within-taxon variation ($\mathbf{G}$, the pooled within-genus covariance matrix across $n = 518$ congeneric species in 62 multi-species genera). Evaluating $\mathbf{g}_{\max}$ versus $\mathbf{r}_{\max}$ before and after decomposing each species into its Global 8p Thompson Field ($\Delta Y_i^{\mathrm{Thompson}}$) plus its Local Modular Residual ($\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_i^{\mathrm{Modular}} = Y_i - Y_i^{\mathrm{Thompson}}$) separates intrinsic morphospace bias from competitive character displacement (\texttt{thompson/run\_morphospace\_vs\_competition\_analysis.py}; Fig.~\ref{fig:morphospace_vs_competition}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Why Raw 34D Landmark Space obscures $\mathbf{G}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{R}$ Alignment ($\theta = 64.6^\circ$, $|\cos\theta| = 0.429$):}
  In unseparated 34D Procrustes space, $\mathbf{g}_{\max}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{\max}$ diverge by $64.6^\circ$ because \textbf{within-genus variation among sympatric congeners ($<15\text{ Myr}$) concentrates $47.0\%$ of its principal variance in the craniofacial/trophic jaw partition ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}2, 11\text{--}17$)}, whereas between-clade macroevolution ($\sim 150\text{ Myr}$) concentrates $\mathbf{85.6\%}$ of its principal variance in the axial body and fin-insertion partition ($\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{--}10$) and only $\mathbf{14.4\%}$ in the cranium. Sympatric sister species thus amplify trophic jaw disparity by $\mathbf{3.26\times}$ ($47.0\%$ vs.\ $14.4\%$) relative to deep-time body-plan radiation, partitioning food resources while maintaining a conserved hydrodynamic body plan.
  \item \textbf{In Global 8p Thompson Morphospace, 150 Myr of Clade Radiation Tracks Within-Genus $\mathbf{G}_{\max}$ ($\theta = 20.7^\circ$, $|\cos\theta| = 0.9352$):}
  Projected into the 8-parameter Thompson coordinate subspace, within-genus $\mathbf{g}_{\max}^{\mathrm{Thompson}}$ and between-clade $\mathbf{r}_{\max}^{\mathrm{Thompson}}$ align with $\mathbf{|\cos\theta| = 0.9352}$ ($\theta = \mathbf{20.7^\circ}$, 2D principal subspace overlap $= \mathbf{86.5\%}$, $p < 10^{-12}$), demonstrating that global body-plan evolution across 150 Myr follows the developmental lines of least resistance captured by Thompson's coordinate fields.
  \item \textbf{In Local Modular Residual Space, Within-Genus Trophic Competition and Macroevolutionary Fin Shifts Are Orthogonal ($\theta = 87.4^\circ$, $|\cos\theta| = 0.0456$):}
  In the local residual space $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{Modular}}$, within-genus $\mathbf{g}_{\max}^{\mathrm{Modular}}$ (trophic jaw character displacement) and between-clade $\mathbf{r}_{\max}^{\mathrm{Modular}}$ (deep-time dorsal/anal fin-insertion shifts) are nearly orthogonal ($87.4^\circ$, $|\cos\theta| = 0.0456$, 2D subspace overlap $= 4.4\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion \& Causal Synthesis: Reconciling Thompson, Bookstein, and Modern Macroevolution}

Why do internal 17-landmark wireframes diverge from a single global coordinate grid above $\sim 45\text{--}50\text{ Myr}$, whereas continuous 600-semilandmark whole-body outlines continue to obey Thompson's 5- to 8-parameter coordinate transformations even across $80\text{--}113\text{ Myr}$ interfamily divergences? Our partition ablation, Relative-Warp, and Phenotypic Trajectory analyses point to three interlocking mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Craniofacial Landmark Density \& Extrapolation Bias ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{--}2, 11\text{--}17$):} Placing $53\%$ of the landmarks inside the anterior $\sim 25\%$ of the fish (the craniofacial partition) weights ordinary least-squares heavily toward trophic remodeling of the jaws and suspensorium: fitting the 9 craniofacial landmarks alone extrapolates across the posterior $75\%$ of the body, producing up to $587.1\%$ posterior silhouette error on \emph{Scarus} $\to$ \emph{Pomacanthus} (and $8{,}925\%$ on \emph{Diodon} $\to$ \emph{Mola}).
  \item \textbf{Evolutionary Modularity Between Craniofacial and Fin-Insertion Partitions:} Embryologically, the teleost skull derives from cranial neural crest cells shaped by trophic mechanics, whereas dorsal, anal, and pelvic fin insertions ($\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{--}10$) shift along somitic myomeres and distal fin webs grow via dermal lepidotrichia~\cite{Satterfield2024,Larouche2018,Zelditch2012,Goswami2014,FeliceGoswami2018,Evans2021}. Even in extreme skeletal apomorphies such as \emph{Mola mola}, where the true caudal fin is lost and replaced by a pseudocaudal \emph{clavus} derived from modified dorsal and anal fin elements (so $\mathrm{LM}\,5\text{--}6$ mark the posterior clavus boundary), the 8-parameter radial-flare grid captures $79.7\%$ of trunk insertion variance while the hypertrophied dorsal/anal fin span scales $2.26\times$ taller ($0.786$ vs.\ $0.349$). Within a genus or young family ($\le 45\text{ Myr}$), global allometric growth gradients scale these modules together ($53\%\text{--}61\%$ within $\sigma_{\mathrm{genus}}$); beyond the $\sim 45\text{--}50\text{ Myr}$ transition, trophic skull evolution and fin-base migration decouple.
  \item \textbf{Locomotor Selection on the Integrated Body-Plus-Median-Fin Planform (and Complementary Utility of Both Protocols):} Ichthyologists intentionally standardized the 17-landmark body-wall protocol because museum specimens vary in fin erection and pinning posture~\cite{ClaverieWainwright2014,Friedman2019}. That protocol remains ideal for posture-invariant skeletal and trophic comparisons. However, in living, swimming fishes (and in the standard lateral taxonomic plates with fully deployed median fins that Thompson selected in 1917), water does not flow around an internal 8-vertex skeletal trunk wireframe --- it flows around the continuous outer planform of the body plus its dorsal, anal, and caudal fins acting as a single integrated hydrofoil~\cite{Webb1984,Wainwright2002}. Because lateral added mass, pressure drag, and turning torque act on the \emph{entire wetted contour}, deep-bodied fishes (\emph{Pomacanthus}, \emph{Antigonia}, \emph{Sternoptyx}, \emph{Mola}) co-adapt their median fins and trunk profile as a unified biomechanical surface --- the exact continuous transformation field that D'Arcy Thompson hypothesized in 1917.
\end{enumerate}
