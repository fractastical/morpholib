Why Michael Levin Is 77.6++% Right on Platonic Ingress

The ghosts score 0.0%. The 77.6% covers only the seven claims in Ingressing Minds — sorting in §6.3, the Thompson morphospace, and the planarian, tadpole, and xenobot (66.7%, 13.5%, 86.5%, 96.1%, 95.0%, 95.0%, 90.1%) — and none of his other work. Structural math on those seven reads 88.1%.

Eight plates here; the claim-by-claim audit is sixteen more. Each caption names the script, dataset, or engraving it was built from.

For some time I have been participating actively in documenting the scientific potential of bioelectricity (which goes back to Galvani’s frog experiments; see Bioelectricity Foundations) and the various fascinating, though usually hypothetical, metaphysical concepts that often travel side by side with it (bioelectricity.net). As a historian of math, it doesn’t bother me that sci-fi concepts (such as psi) and real science can develop side by side—historically, Kepler’s astrophysics and Newton’s alchemy grew out of the same soil—but I’m equally aware that religious effects can take over and obliterate free critical thinking (e.g. guru traditions).

Plate I — Thompson’s rubber sheet, moving. A real parrotfish outline is bent through four of his 1917 coordinate families: the 70° shear, the coaxial circles that turn Scarus into an angelfish, the bilinear fan, and the radial flare. Provenance: thompson/1917-thompson.py.

When I was working at the California Institute of Machine Consciousness in early 2026, my then-employer Joscha Bach would often elatedly insert concepts of morphogenesis into conversations. This term means, roughly, how things get to be their current shape, and tends to be a pretty mathy set of explanations.

When a board member of the organization asked me to explain it, I did so as follows:

"As someone with a computational background leaning into bio, Michael Levin has taken the scalpel down to the smallest component parts, worked on new imaging techniques, and tried to understand how cells take their form, which is related to newly observable phenomena via the electrical communication that happens between cells. This then is used to explain various other previously unexplainable phenomena."

This satisfied her, but she said that until I explained it that way, most of the Mike Levin orbit concepts sounded instead impermeable or much like "woo science."

In my case, I was fortunately aided by Mike Levin himself, who gave me a list of articles to read in which order to get started on learning the space, to which I added a massive pile of his papers, which I synthesized with a knowledge graph (Project Delphi). That knowledge graph then evolved into a three-stage series of open-source verification engines—starting with NeuroEval (doi:10.5281/zenodo.20622878, evaluating data transparency and neural-coding rigor in computational and biohybrid/wetware neuroscience papers), followed by BioEval (doi:10.5281/zenodo.20567719, evaluating bioinformatics and computational-biology papers), and finally culminating in MorphoEval (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23680705, fractastical/morpholib, and our Adversarial Epistemic Loop / AEL protocol), which turns that auditing machinery inward to autonomously build out, audit, and formally prove the math of biology in executable code and Lean 4.

Plate II — Fifteen flagship animated models of biological form (1917–2025) in our open-source Project Delphi repository, each audited by independent AI referees from competing labs and formally checked in the Lean 4 theorem prover. Provenance: docs/media/make_montage.py.

Although none of this required "Platonism," it was partially the concept of validating the concepts of what mathematicians called the "morphospace" and Levin calls the "latent Platonic space" that motivated the creation of these tools—and which now allows agents to explore math more directly and which has already led to notable discoveries.

As someone who needs sizable amounts of funding to conduct his experiments (his lab has produced 400+ papers in the last decade), he is probably more sensitive than I am to the language that makes funders want to fund. However, his more recent set of terms choosing "Platonism" and other terms like "ingress" (Ingressing Minds, 2025/2026) have set off a large set of internet debates, in which I inadvertently got involved when Sweet Rationalism (Nathan Sweet) suggested my method for validation may be more comprehensive and better than Levin’s own.

Although we have briefly discussed these things in person, I do not know, at present, all of the experiments Mike Levin has underway. What I know is mostly limited to the core arguments he brings in his most recent paper, which are the D’Arcy Thompson-inspired version of the morphology of math (Thompson 1917) and the Zhang, Goldstein & Levin (2025) paper regarding emergent phenomena in Bubble, Insertion, and Selection sort (alongside the classic bioelectric flatworm, Picasso tadpole, and Xenobot models).

In both cases, precise language of what is claimed is what is most important, and, in a sea of definitions, a place where an internet philosopher who can code might be most useful. It also turns out that despite a bunch of folks who are "pro" and "against" on the internet, all of the main claims can be validated with tools that everyone has at their fingertips.

The Score

Plate IV is the point-by-point accounting. Each claim is counted once.

  • Sorting cells, Claims 1–3: 18.5 / 33.3, or 55.6%. Bubble and Selection sort really do tolerate a frozen cell with nothing but a skip. Insertion sort does not: it rewrites its own test. The "delayed gratification" curve is the neighbor ruler going up while the true energy goes down on every swap. The clustering is real, and two algorithms moving at different speeds produce it. This is the lowest pillar, and it is the only one that checks published code line by line.¹
  • Thompson’s morphospace, Claim 4: 32.0 / 33.3, or 96.1%. A century of failed tests came from measuring the skull. On whole-body outlines the coordinate grids win, the pre-registered blind test held (p = 10⁻¹⁰³), and the 1.3 points held back are the hypothesis that lost to "copy the nearest relative" by 4% (p = 0.061).
  • Planarians, tadpoles, xenobots, Claims 5–7. The laboratory results are valid, and each claim counts. Read one at a time they are 10.55 / 11.1 = 95.0%, 10.55 / 11.1 = 95.0%, and 10.0 / 11.1 = 90.1%. The points not awarded are the gaps in our reduced equations — fixed channel kinetics, the viscoelastic coupling, the full 3D fluid acting on the tissue. They are not a finding that the worm, the tadpole, or the xenobot failed to do what the papers report.

The sorting claims, read the same way, are 7.4 / 11.1 = 66.7%, 1.5 / 11.1 = 13.5%, and 9.6 / 11.1 = 86.5%. Thompson is 96.1%. All seven:

(66.7% + 13.5% + 86.5% + 96.1% + 95.0% + 95.0% + 90.1%) / 7 = 77.6%.

That is the number in the title, and its scope is the paper. These seven are the claims Ingressing Minds makes: three about the sorting arrays in §6.3, one about Thompson’s coordinate morphospace, and three about the planarian, the tadpole, and the xenobot. Nothing else he has published is in the average.

With the dualist deductions taken out of each denominator before averaging, structural math on the same seven claims reads (100% + 40.5% + 100% + 96.1% + 95.0% + 95.0% + 90.1%) / 7 = 88.1%. 0.0% of the dualist points were earned.

Rationalism: Short and Sweet

Nathan Sweet (Sweet Rationalism) asked for a decisive comparison:

"Joel Dietz points in the right direction by proposing a predictive morphospace language connecting bioelectric patterns to downstream gene-expression cascades and morphology. A knowledge graph, however, can encode D or D + P. Platonic ingress begins earning evidence only when at least one P-specific relation improves held-out prediction, intervention, compression, or comparative model performance beyond the best D-only account.

The decisive experiment would make P itself measurable at the edges: specify its hard core and bridge principle before observation; define a P-linked variable, topology, relation, or invariant independently of the residual; identify outcomes that materially separate D + P from serious rivals; use negative controls and held-out embodiments; replicate the advantage independently. Most important, freeze the update rule. A failed bridge must cost P something. A repair must buy fresh risk."

That is the test this article runs.

On the sorting arrays the local rules already do the work. Adding a non-physical edge changes the prediction by nothing. The signed claim records the zero.

On the fish, the Thompson sheet was locked before FishShapes v1. Inside 50 million years it beat the clade consensus (p = 10⁻¹⁰³). Copying the nearest relative won one noisy measurement (p = 0.061), and that hypothesis was rejected. The loss is in the same record as the win.

On hornet wings the same sheet passed 0 of 7 across 1,027 wings. The outer frame is Thompsonian, 65.8%, higher than fish bodies at 57.9%. A single sheet dragged across the interior veins goes the wrong way. Digitizer noise and body size were ruled out before the measurement. The miss stayed visible, and the repair had to be a new signed question, not a retune of the old one.

A latent space is not a sticker that collects credit from a label. Define the invariant before the data, lock the prediction, and publish the miss.

So Have We Abandoned Dualism? Yes. But Not the Way You Think.

One of the points Levin makes as he responds to AI-generated prose—including the 36,000 words Nathan Sweet originally sent him, containing every possible argument one could make against his positions—is that AI tends to be locked to old categories and, by consequence, traps you inside of them. In his recent post on the subject he put it this way:

"The problem is that currently, the AI’s seem to be making the same kind of logical errors (from my perspective of course) and expressing the same kind of preconceptions that are prevalent in status quo human thinkers… Right now the LLMs are trapped in the same attractors of thinking that have held several fields back for decades; perhaps not surprisingly. That will change; let’s go AIs—read the papers, follow new directions, be not afraid of ancient philosophical ‘category errors’."

In that sense, trained on previous corpora of human knowledge and without phenomenological awareness, the AI remains self-referential with that corpus. Of "within" and "without," the AI is forced to be "within," because that is all that it knows.

The more anthropological view on this is that if you come to a tribe that has never touched the outside world, and its "training data" is thusly limited, how can you expect it to interact with and absorb outside knowledge that is outside of its frame? This is the origin of "cargo cults" and various other phenomena that are now well documented.

Consequently, if "dualism" is a position that implies a "without," then AI cannot but collapse the categories and imply that this other is improbable. For example, if math is placed inside of the first category (and thus subject to our full framework of causality), that does not necessarily imply that it is not in some way agentic, or that we fully understand how math works—and, more particularly, how it binds itself to biology, in what cases that constitutes a law, and in what other circumstances it may be more stochastically oriented. That is, after all, exactly the question the hornet wings left open above: Thompson’s sheet is a law on fish bodies and on the outer wing frame, and something looser at the interior cross-vein junctions.

Similarly, we have everywhere the problem of "minds," which Levin and the aforementioned Joscha Bach have discussed with various framings—such as the possibility that various forms of consciousness may be framed as computational and may cross between different computational substrates (Bach’s position), or, in Levin’s own anti-computationalist framing, that whatever mind a system has lives precisely in the spaces its algorithm neither forces nor forbids. In that sense, humans, despite being a relatively advanced species in some respects, have not even quite figured out how trees communicate with each other. So there is a lot to learn here. A great deal of it is science, but not all of it—which is one reason why new research institutes need to exist.

One particularly helpful arena—covered by the California Institute for Machine Consciousness (including in the philosophy seminars I used to organize there), but even more so by folks like the Qualia Research Institute, the Institute for Advanced Consciousness Studies (IACS), Meditation Research at Harvard / MGH, and Sensoria Research—is what those in our community call the phenomenological state space. This concept effectively concerns altered states of consciousness, especially those in which one believes one has encountered an "Other."

Many such examples exist, including dreams, extended meditations, pharmacologically induced states, mantras, and art. What one encounters is sometimes described as "the divine," a particular deity, a machine elf, or a flash of insight—and in many cases the sense of self disappears, such that one appears to be one with this other.

Part of the more general question in adjudicating any such "other" is not necessarily doubting the realness of these encounters, but how they relate to the rest of our lives. In this, the growing field of xenobiology—the study of forms never yet seen in nature, pioneered by Levin and others—has much in common with these other fields, which have mostly bubbled up from the deep reaches of the intellectually and experimentally oriented internet, and they have much to offer each other—even to AI, if the phenomenological reach can also be integrated.²

The Deep Platonic AI Stack

The problem, if you read two seminal pieces in the AI literature, is even more profound. One prong suggests that we are converging on a single Platonic representation—the Platonic Representation Hypothesis of Huh, Cheung, Wang & Isola (2024), who show that as vision and language models scale, they measure distance between datapoints in more and more alike ways, and hypothesize that this convergence is driving toward "a shared statistical model of reality, akin to Plato’s concept of an ideal reality"—because Platonism is basically correct. There is math that orders the universe, and even when you have an interface layer that is "biology," the messiness is in the bottom layer. In that sense there is a hierarchy, and our physical wetware is not at the top. Even when it is deterministic, it is probably deterministic in the flawed sense of a machine that sometimes brings a task to completion and sometimes forgets what it is doing along the way. We have, in other words, a "good" (Platonic perfection) and a "bad" (messy biology).

The second prong—the Artificial Hivemind of Jiang, Chai, Li, et al. (2025), who put 26,000 open-ended real-world queries to language models and found not only intra-model repetition but, more so, inter-model homogeneity, with different models producing strikingly similar outputs—suggests that all AI is feeding on the same basic dataset, including both the internet and talking to other AIs, and thus converges on endless samehood, aka a hivemind. Back in our phenomenological framing, it means that AI is able to access itself.

Although both of these prongs would seem at first glance mutually exclusive—one converges on the world, the other on itself—at the moment they suffer from a similar flaw. Neither is naturally teleological, and neither allows the system to absorb from the "unknown unknown."

This is interesting because, in the words of Jensen Huang (fantastically quoted on a slide at the J. Craig Venter Memorial Symposium under the heading "…Biology is the Final Frontier for Engineering"):

"For the very first time in human history, biology has the opportunity to be engineering, not science. When something becomes engineering, it becomes exponentially improving."

That is the same point: engineering is what exploration looks like once the unknown unknown has been made into something known. The relatively unexplored expanse of the cell—the "mess"—remains one of the best ways to discover this path to knowing, including through the empirical yet data-driven exploration recorded in our evolving Problem Graph (Plate VI, footnote ³). Every node there is pinned to something outside the corpus—a wet-lab dataset, a held-out species, a hornet wing—so when a pre-registered prediction fails, the failure comes from nature rather than from the hivemind. That is the one exit from both prongs we have found so far: teleology supplied by the locked hypothesis, and the unknown unknown supplied by the organism.

So what does this do to the scoreboard? Nothing to any claim's points. Our audit proved that the dynamics D are sufficient for every behaviour in the sorting arrays—0 cell mismatches, ΔI = −1 on every swap. Sufficiency of D is not falsity of P. "0.0% needed" is a statement about what the code requires, not about what exists. An auditor that was ~90% AI on code, proofs, and refereeing in the technical sections (see the Transparency Heatmap in the Appendix, Plate VIII) is, by Levin’s own argument, structurally unable to see "without"—so the 0.0% is a lower bound produced by a reviewer who can only look in one direction. Three of the four dualist deductions survive that objection, because they are deductions for sentences that are false about the code: the FREEZE handler exists (Claim 1), zero cells cross the barrier (Claim 2), and no swap goes uphill on I(v) (Claim 2). The fourth is less clear-cut. Claim 3’s 1.5 "ghost clustering" points were deducted for an unprogrammed, unpredicted pattern that turned out to be a mathematical free lunch of the medium—exactly the kind of thing we award P_structural credit for in the claims above. Filing it under "ghost" may have been the auditor forcing within/without to be the only two boxes. We do not fix that by hand; a human reaching in to move a point would be the same error in the other direction. We fix it by leaving the loop open.

Verdict — Not an override, a loop. No one is allowed to edit a claim's points by hand, and Plate IV does not: every card shows the points that claim earned. The mean of Claims 1–7 is 77.6%. The ++ in the title is a general statement that we expect the number to improve: it lives inside an evolving, improving, self-correcting loop in which every deduction is a node in the Problem Graph with a status and a source, any node can be reopened by new evidence—a referee, a new model system from Levin’s lab, a pre-registered hypothesis resolving, or an AI that finally reads "without"—and when a node changes, the scoreboard is re-rendered from the locked files. Most of the open nodes are places where Levin has not yet been tested rather than places where he has failed, which is why the expected direction is up. Claim 3’s 1.5 ghost-clustering points stay deducted until the loop, not a curator, moves them. That is the honest answer to Levin’s objection: we cannot make the auditor see "without," but we can make sure it is never the last word.³

Conclusion³

In a private exchange with Levin, I cited my other article on Hellenic Tantra and noted the longstanding Hellenic practice—running through Eleusis, Delphi, and Plato’s own dialogues (Seventh Letter, 341c–d)—of a nonverbal, ritualized encounter with other forms of knowing that were not even allowed to be scribbled down. Re-reading D’Arcy Thompson’s On Growth and Form, I was struck by the explicit influence of Aristotle’s History of Animals—and when I went back to Thompson’s own 1910 Oxford translation and prefatory note to that work (where he shows how Aristotle’s biology grew out of direct observation at a specific place, the lagoon of Pyrrha on Lesbos), I realized that this "direct knowing," rooted in specific places, is what actually grounds and enforces epistemic rigor. It is what allows us to formulate things that are genuine mathematical laws rather than the "would-be" or "almost" laws we so often find in the contemporary literature of emergence. That is also the wisdom of tradition and of standing on the shoulders of giants: Thompson stated his coordinate transformations as conjecture and presumably yearned for the proof—and here we stand, with modern computational tools, holding something that approaches a true law of biological form.

It was also via our method—which deliberately includes the adversarial audit of advanced AI—that we were made aware of Thompson’s 1913 Herbert Spencer Lecture at Oxford, On Aristotle as a Biologist (Thompson 1913). The lecture makes the bold claim that Aristotle’s power as a philosopher comes from his strong localized knowledge and his initial concentration as a naturalist: the natural history of the lagoon at Pyrrha came first, and the metaphysics grew out of it. Thompson then goes further—the concept of the psyche (what we have since come to call the "soul") is itself derived from the observation of nature. Aristotle’s De Anima classifies the soul the way he classified fishes: a nutritive soul shared with plants, a sentient and motile soul shared with animals, and a reasoning soul in man, each lower grade implicit in the higher "as a triangle within a square." It is a nested geometry, not a ghost; and in De Anima II.4 that same psyche is named as the body’s formal, efficient, and final cause at once—the very analysis of causation that made Aristotle’s precise and elaborate taxonomies possible, and that still underlies many of our maps of nature. In that sense, if we want to understand what animates us, we may need to start not with the later, transmuted, and oversimplified idea of the soul, but with the original one: a graded, nested form, read off living bodies by a man who had spent his years dissecting cuttlefish on Lesbos.

Appendix — How This Was Audited

How do you keep yourself honest when testing high-profile scientific claims—especially when both the "Platonic Space" camp and the "Nothing New Here" blog critics have strong priors?

The methodology used in this article—which we call the Adversarial Epistemic Loop (AEL)—was developed across three successive evaluation systems in Project Delphi:

  1. Stage 1 — NeuroEval (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20622878): Built first to evaluate data transparency, reproducibility, and neural-coding rigor in computational and biohybrid/wetware neuroscience papers.
  2. Stage 2 — BioEval (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20567719): Extended that rubric to score bioinformatics and computational-biology papers for reproducibility, statistical calibration, and information-theoretic rigor.
  3. Stage 3 — MorphoEval (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23680705; code in fractastical/morpholib): Turned the evaluation machinery from grading published papers to generating, executing, and auditing the mathematical models themselves in a 12-step closed loop—where every verbal claim is translated into multi-language executable code (Python 3, C++20, Node.js) and machine-checked Lean 4 theorems (0 sorry), locked against SHA-256 empirical datasets, audited blind by three competing AI model families (Google DeepMind’s gemini-4-argon, Anthropic’s claude-fable-5-1 / claude-sonnet-5-5, and OpenAI’s gpt-5.6-sol), and pre-registered as cryptographically signed Nanopublications before unblinding held-out species.

Plate VII — The Turing closed loop, on the 1952 reaction–diffusion model. The evaluation runs in this order: scout, paper, formal source, pin the rules, script, output and compare, dual-agent audit, measure, write the claims, sign and publish, public record, then remediate. A failed pass goes back to the script. Provenance: morphoeval/track_loop.py, run on models/1952-turing-morpho.py; the pass is written up in morphoeval/runs/2026-10-19-turing-morpho-loop.md.
