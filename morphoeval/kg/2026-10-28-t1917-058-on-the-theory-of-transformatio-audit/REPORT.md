## Findings and remediation

### Thompson (1917) Cartesian transformation of the human skull into chimpanzee, baboon and dog (Figs. 404-408)  —  overall 62.1 (mostly faithful)

v1 reconstructs Thompson's human->chimpanzee net as a TPS (bending energy 2.10, affine determinant 0.54, net-alignment RMS 1.02 grid units) and warps the Fig. 404 drawing through it, visually reproducing Fig. 406. Applied to the 11 human landmarks of Fig. 404 the net removes 35 % of the Procrustes distance to a real chimpanzee skull (0.383 -> 0.249; affine part alone 0.430, i.e. -12 %), 24 % toward the baboon, 22 % toward the dog and -27 % toward the human specimen (T is a prognathism direction that helps every non-human target, most for the chimpanzee). Direction cosine between Thompson's drawn displacement and the real human->chimp displacement 0.73. Cell-area field of the net: log-linear R^2 0.95, beta_i +0.40 (behind->forwards), beta_j -0.61 (upwards), 5.7x front/back, 7.4x bottom/top. Real specimens: d_P(H,C) 0.315 < d_P(H,B) 0.505 < d_P(H,D) 0.603; cos(chimp,baboon) 0.78, cos(chimp,dog) 0.78, k_baboon/k_chimp 1.25; facial index 0.48/0.66/1.27/0.61 and facial angle 89/68/56/45 deg for H/C/B/D. Verdict by the script's 11 binary checks: 9/11 ("supported") - but without any uncertainty quantification. Overall 62.2/100.

- **Model fidelity to source: 70** — Thompson's method is implemented as published - a Cartesian net of the human skull and its smooth deformation - using the standard modern formalisation (Bookstein 1989 TPS: exact interpolation of the drawn nodes, affine + bending decomposition), and the net itself is Thompson's own Fig. 405 rather than a re-drawn analogue. Four Lean 4 laws (affine cell congruence, log-area gradient, intensity family, rectangular-vs-triangular taper) are kernel-checked, 0 sorry. Deductions: the baboon and dog nets (Figs. 407-408) are not digitised, so Thompson's own drawn transformations for those taxa are not reconstructed; the TPS regularisation lambda and intensity k are CLI options but never swept in v1.
