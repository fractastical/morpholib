## How a body gets its form

Morphogenesis is how a living thing gets its shape, and regeneration is how it gets it back: cells divide, move and signal until a body plan appears, or reappears after a cut. The classic models of form reproduced here are geometric ([Thompson's transformations](#thompson1917-thompsonpy), [Raup's coiling shells](#1966-rauppy), [Vogel's golden-angle sunflower head](#1979-vogel-phyllotaxispy), [Runions's auxin space-colonisation leaf veins](#2005-runions-leaf-venationpy)), chemical ([Turing's reacting and diffusing morphogens](#1952-turing-morphopy), [Meinhardt's shell pigmentation beside Wolfram's Rule 30](#1984-wolfram-conus-textile-rule30py)) or physical ([coral](#2013-chindapol-coral-flowpy) and [stromatolite](#2010-petroff-stromatolitepy) growth shaped by flow and light). The newer idea most of the rest turn on is that the pattern is also stored and read out *bioelectrically*: every cell holds a resting voltage across its membrane, neighbouring cells share it through gap junctions, and the resulting voltage map across a tissue tells it where a head, a tail or a limb belongs. Change the voltage and the form changes — block the junctions and a planarian regrows two heads. That is the experiment the [Planform](#2013-lobo-planform-morphologypy) and [Limbform](#2014-lobo-limbform-morphologypy) databases record, hundreds of variants at a time, as formal graphs of what was cut, what was blocked and what grew back, and that the [Cervera–Levin–Mafe](#2021-cerveralevinmafepy) model puts into equations. The repository turns such papers into running programs whose output *is* the form; MorphoEval then scores how faithfully each program follows its paper.

### Discovery I — Resolving the 109-Year Paradox in D'Arcy Thompson's 1917 Fish Transformations

📄 **Paper PDF:** [`docs/thompson_1917_fish_transformations_manuscript.pdf`](./docs/thompson_1917_fish_transformations_manuscript.pdf) · **Full Explanation:** [`docs/KEY_DISCOVERIES.md#discovery-i`](./docs/KEY_DISCOVERIES.md#discovery-i--resolving-the-109-year-paradox-in-darcy-thompsons-1917-fish-transformations) · **Scripts:** [`thompson/1917-thompson-silhouette-area-paradox.py`](./thompson/1917-thompson-silhouette-area-paradox.py), [`thompson/1917-thompson-four-families-atlas.py`](./thompson/1917-thompson-four-families-atlas.py), [`thompson/run_rigor_stress_tests.py`](./thompson/run_rigor_stress_tests.py)

![Animated Summary of D'Arcy Thompson's 1917 Fish Transformations: The 17-Landmark vs. 600-Point Silhouette Paradox](./docs/media/thompson_1917_silhouette_paradox_summary.gif)

* **The 17-Landmark Skeletal Wireframe vs. 600-Semilandmark Whole-Body Outline Discrepancy:** Why did modern landmark morphometricians conclude that D'Arcy Thompson's (1917, Figs. 373–382) smooth 5- to 8-parameter coordinate grids fail across fish families? Because the standard 17-landmark reef-fish protocol (Claverie & Wainwright 2014; Friedman et al. 2019) places **$9/17$ landmarks ($53\%$ of least-squares weight) inside the craniofacial partition ($\mathrm{LM}\,1\text{–}2, 11\text{–}17$)** and the remaining $8/17$ at basal fin-ray insertions along the body wall ($\mathrm{LM}\,3\text{–}10$), leaving **$0\%$ on the distal dorsal, anal, and caudal fin margins or ventral body profile**. Consequently, fitting global grids (or even exact 34-parameter Thin-Plate Splines) to the 17 landmarks on interfamily pairs **actively degrades the real whole-body silhouette compared to no non-rigid warp at all**: non-overlapping silhouette area $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$ worsens from **$40.6\% \to 56.2\%$** on *Scarus* $\to$ *Pomacanthus* ($\mathrm{IoU}: 0.662 \to 0.618$, or $0.629$ under 34p TPS), **$58.4\% \to 63.0\%$** on *Argyropelecus* $\to$ *Sternoptyx*, and **$102.2\% \to 143.7\%$** on *Scorpaena* $\to$ *Antigonia*.
* **Direct 600-Semilandmark Outline Fitting Vindicates Thompson (1917):** Fitting the **exact same 5- to 8-parameter Thompson coordinate families** (I. 5p Oblique Shear, II. 8p Complex Möbius Coaxial Circles, III. 8p Bilinear Fan, IV. 8p Hyperbolic/Radial Expansion) directly to the 600-semilandmark whole-body outlines—anchored only at Bookstein's two-point snout-to-peduncle baseline ($\mathrm{LM}\,2$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{LM}\,5+\mathrm{LM}\,6)$) to prevent tangential sliding—recovers every single 1917 transformation with **$100.0\%$ diffeomorphic bijectivity ($\det D\Phi(x,y) > 0$ everywhere, zero grid folding)** and **bidirectional $S \leftrightarrow T$ invertibility**: $|\Omega_A \Delta \Omega_B|$ drops to **$28.5\%$** ($\mathrm{IoU}=0.749$) on *Scarus* $\to$ *Pomacanthus*, **$17.2\%$** ($\mathrm{IoU}=0.839$) on *Polyprion* $\to$ *Pristigenys*, **$35.5\%$** ($3.4\times$ reduction from $120.1\%$) on *Pristigenys* $\to$ *Scorpaena*, **$57.6\%$** ($2.5\times$ reduction from $143.7\%$) on *Scorpaena* $\to$ *Antigonia*, **$45.6\%$** on *Argyropelecus* $\to$ *Sternoptyx*, and **$49.0\%$** on *Diodon* $\to$ *Mola mola*.
* **The $\sim 45\text{–}50\text{ Myr}$ Evolutionary Disparity Transition Across 791 Species (`4,334` Dated Pairs):** Across 4,334 time-calibrated divergences on the Rabosky et al. (2018) megaphylogeny, 8-parameter Thompson grids pull 17-landmark Procrustes RMS residuals down inside the intrageneric Procrustes disparity band ($\sigma_{\text{genus}} \le 0.0110$) in **$53.0\%\text{–}61.3\%$** of pairs below $50\text{ Myr}$ (beating the 6p uniform affine component in **$82.2\%$** and Bookstein's 12p quadratic trend surface in **$84.9\%\text{–}92.1\%$** of pairs by small-sample $\mathrm{AIC}_c$), before transitioning at **$t^* = 45.8\text{ Myr}$** ($n=187$ phylogenetically independent node-disjoint pairs: $54.0\%$ within $\sigma_{\text{genus}}$ at $<50\text{ Myr}$ vs. **$0.0\%$** at $\ge 50\text{ Myr}$) to **$10.6\%$** ($50\text{–}80\text{ Myr}$) and **$0.0\%$** ($>110\text{ Myr}$) as median/paired fin-base insertions decouple from the craniofacial module ($\mathrm{Fin/Head\ RMS} = 1.16\times \to 1.28\times \to 1.48\times$; Satterfield, Claverie & Wainwright 2024).
* **Comparison Against Standard 600-Semilandmark TPS/EFD & Phylomorphospace Convergence ([`thompson/run_wainwright_600pt_and_convergence_tests.py`](./thompson/run_wainwright_600pt_and_convergence_tests.py), [`docs/outreach_fig4_wainwright_600pt_and_convergence.png`](./docs/outreach_fig4_wainwright_600pt_and_convergence.png)):** On the **exact same 600-semilandmark outlines** across all 6 pairs, standard 600-semilandmark curve Thin-Plate Splines (Bookstein 1997) and 12p quadratic trend surfaces **fold the 2D interior coordinate grid** ($\det D\Phi \le 0$ on `6/6` and `4/6` pairs, with $\min \det D\Phi$ down to $-6.76$) and degrade **out-of-sample prediction of held-out interior skeletal landmarks by up to $2.4\times$**, whereas 8p Thompson grids are **$100\%$ globally diffeomorphic (`6/6` pairs)** and accurately predict held-out internal anatomy from the outer outline alone. Across all 791 species (Leave-One-Family-Out CV across 48 families), Thompson's 1917 analytical laws capture **$92.0\%$ (median) / $95.6\%$ (mean) of the optimal 8-parameter Empirical Relative-Warp variance (Rohlf 1993)** (`50.9%` vs. `55.3%`, compared to `25.1%` for the 6p uniform component) and **$88.6\%$ of the primary `(PC1, PC2)` phylomorphospace plane (Sidlauskas 2008)**, while **Phenotypic Trajectory Analysis (Adams & Collyer 2009)** shows that distantly related families (`> 95 Myr` apart) converging on deep-bodied or elongate/fusiform morphotypes evolve along **strongly parallel 8p Thompson coordinate trajectories ($r_{\text{vec}} = +0.693$ and $+0.714$, vs. $-0.699$ between divergent clades)** with distinct "many-to-one" craniofacial and fin-insertion offsets (Wainwright et al. 2005).

## Part 1 — How It Works: The 12-Step MorphoEval + Lean 4 Loop

### The 12-Step Closed Loop

![How a reproduction is made and how MorphoEval scores it](./docs/media/morphoeval_pipeline.gif)

*From paper to reproduction to signed evaluation — the MorphoEval pipeline ([animated GIF](./docs/media/morphoeval_pipeline.gif) · [high-res PNG](./docs/media/morphoeval_pipeline.png) · [Mermaid source](./morphoeval/README.md#the-cycle)).*

Every simulation in this repository is built and evaluated through a **12-step closed loop** split into two tiers:

- **Tier A — Making a reproduction (Steps 1–6):**
  1. **`1 Scout` (👤 + 🤖):** Pick a classic paper, an open empirical dataset, or a quantitative biological claim worth testing.
  2. **`2 Paper` (🤖):** Extract the exact governing equations, algorithmic rules, and empirical reference tables from the primary literature.
  3. **`3 Formal source` (🤖):** Vendor or cache the empirical tables and specimen photographs in `datasets/<name>/` and lock every file by **SHA-256** so any altered input is refused.
  4. **`4 Pin the rules` (🤖 + Lean 4):** Formalize the paper's core mathematical invariants as machine-checked theorems in [`morphoeval/lean/MathOfBiology.lean`](./morphoeval/lean/MathOfBiology.lean) (`0 sorry`, verified by the Lean 4 kernel in ~1 s) and test them on every record.
  5. **`5 Script` (🤖 builder):** Write one self-contained Python program (`<year>-<author>-<model>.py`) that reproduces the model, decodes the formal graph, or tests the claim, complete with deterministic `--self-test` assertions.
  6. **`6 Output & compare` (tool):** Render the static comparison figure (`.png`), growth/morphospace animation (`.gif` in `docs/media/`), and quantitative morphometrics table (`.csv`), placing simulated outputs directly beside segmented real specimens.
- **Tier B — Auditing it & closing the remediation loop (Steps 7–12):**
  7. **`7 Dual-agent audit` (🤖🤖 assessors from different companies):** Two independent LLM assessors from different providers (`claude-fable-5-1` / `claude-sonnet-5-5-high` from Anthropic, `gemini-4-argon` from Google, `gpt-5.6-sol` from OpenAI) score the script across **7 weighted dimensions (0–100)** with commit-pinned `#L<a>-L<b>` code loci:
     - **Model-implementation fidelity (`impl.`, weight 30):** `model-fidelity` (20) + `parameter-expressivity` (10)
     - **Biological/empirical validation (`bio.`, weight 40):** `specimen-match` (25) + `morphometric-validation` (15)
     - **Reproducibility (`repro.`, weight 30):** `source-traceability` (10) + `code-traceability` (8) + `repro-package` (12)
  8. **`8 Measure` (`measure_outputs.py`):** Hash rendered outputs (`spec_fingerprint` + `artifact_sha256`) and independently compute morphometric residuals against the empirical data in `morphoeval/audits/<audit>.measurements.yaml`.
  9. **`9 Write as claims` (`build_morphoeval_kg.py`):** Compile the audit and measurements into governed [Project Delphi Space](https://w3id.org/spaces/delphi) RDF nanopublications (`pk:Claim` + `pk:LLMClaimAssessment`).
  10. **`10 Sign & publish` (`sign_morphoeval.py` + `check_signed.mjs`):** Cryptographically sign every `.trig` nanopublication with RSA-2048 keys, mint immutable trusty URIs (`https://w3id.org/np/RA…`), and verify signatures via `@nanopub/sign`.
  11. **`11 Public record` (`published.json` + `REPORT.md`):** Commit the signed nanopubs and SPARQL summary report under `morphoeval/kg/<audit>/`.
  12. **`12 Remediate` (👤 + 🤖 loop back to Step 5):** Every defect found by the independent second assessor becomes an explicit checklist item. The builder re-works Step 5 (and Step 4 Lean proofs), regenerates Step 6 outputs, re-audits in Step 7, and signs a new set of nanopublications that point to the Pass 1 nanopubs via `npx:supersedes` (nothing published is ever overwritten).

### [models/2005-runions-leaf-venation.py](./models/2005-runions-leaf-venation.py)

Runions et al.'s (2005) auxin space-colonisation model of leaf venation, paired with Murray's (1926) minimum-work vascular power law, Price, Knox & Brodribb's (2013) Horton–Strahler transition across 349 cleared leaves (1,514,771 vein junctions), and Katifori & Magnasco's (2012) hierarchical loop-nesting metric on real and simulated leaf networks:
1. **Open (dendritic, *Ginkgo biloba*) vs. closed (reticulate dicot) space colonisation (Runions et al. 2005, Eqs. 1–3, Table 1):** In open venation (`load_table1_regime("14")`), each auxin source $s$ activates only its nearest vein node $v$ within perception radius $b_d$, producing an acyclic dichotomous tree ($V = 735, E = 734, L = E - V + 1 = 0$). In closed venation (`load_table1_regime("15")` and `"10"`), each source $s$ activates its **Relative Neighborhood Graph (RNG)** candidate set $U(s)$ — all vein nodes within $b_d$ not shadowed inside the open Toussaint lune $\|s-w\| < \|s-v\| \land \|v-w\| < \|s-v\|$ — and maintains a persistent tagged set $V(s)$ until the converging vein tips meet at $s$ (with genealogical hop exclusion `tree_hop_distance >= 4` and exact planar segment-intersection rejection `segments_properly_intersect`), producing a planar loopy network ($V = 1,099, E = 1,507$, **$L = 409$ cycles**, **358 enclosed interior dual areoles**, `0` crossing edges).
2. **Murray's (1926) vascular power law & Price et al.'s (2013) Horton–Strahler transition:** Bottom-up pipe-model accumulation $r_{\text{parent}}^\gamma = \sum_{c} r_c^\gamma$ (`compute_murray_radii`, residual $0.00 \times 10^0$), plus an 8-order multiscale vascular tree (`build_and_measure_multiscale_strahler_tree`, $2,175$ nodes, $252$ binary bifurcations) whose measured daughter-to-parent ratios $\alpha_3 = (r_1^3+r_2^3)/r_p^3$ and $\alpha_2 = (r_1^2+r_2^2)/r_p^2$ reproduce Price et al.'s (2013) empirical transition from Murray's cube law ($\gamma = 3$ in minor orders 1–2, $\alpha_3 = 0.961$ vs empirical $0.946$) to area-preserving branching ($\gamma = 2$ in major orders 5–6, $\alpha_2 = 0.996$ vs empirical $0.990$; Pearson **$r = 0.9679$**, **$MAE = 0.0208$**).
3. **Pixel segmentation & planar dual areole nesting on empirical specimens (`segment_empirical_specimens`, `compute_planar_areole_nesting_from_raster`):** Both vendored photographs in [`datasets/venation/`](./datasets/venation/) are segmented directly from pixels — extracting **168 enclosed planar areoles** ($CV = 1.6626$) and Katifori loop-nesting index **$i_u = 0.6134$** from the cleared dicot vein skeleton (`reticulate_vein_skeleton_specimen.jpg`) alongside **358 enclosed planar areoles** and **$i_u = 0.4462$** on the simulated closed network (vs. $i_u = 0.2512$ on a uniform grid control, $\Delta i_u = +0.1950$), and **1,651,010 lamina pixels** with parallel dichotomous ridge density $0.4383$ ($L = 0$) on the *Ginkgo biloba* leaf (`ginkgo_open_venation_specimen.jpg`).

### [models/1979-vogel-phyllotaxis.py](./models/1979-vogel-phyllotaxis.py)

### [thompson/1917-thompson-scarus-pomacanthus.py](./thompson/1917-thompson-scarus-pomacanthus.py)

Thompson's *Scarus* → *Pomacanthus* transformation (*On Growth and Form* 1917, p. 749, Figs. 375–376: "deform its rectilinear co-ordinates into a system of (approximately) coaxial circles … we obtain a very good outline of an allied fish") tested on real species rather than on his drawings. Thompson drew the grids by hand and left no way to fit them; a Cartesian grid whose lines become two pencils of coaxial circles is the image of the plane under a Möbius map, so his claim for this pair is a six-constant hypothesis that can be fitted by least squares to homologous landmarks. The script uses the 17 lateral-view landmarks of Claverie & Wainwright (2014) for 791 Indo-Pacific shore fishes (Friedman et al. 2019, Dryad, CC0, vendored and hash-checked in [`datasets/thompson/`](./datasets/thompson/)) — 22 *Scarus*, 9 *Pomacanthus* and 54 *Chaetodon* species — Procrustes-superimposes them, and fits the *Scarus* genus mean to the *Pomacanthus* genus mean with a similarity, an affine map, the Möbius map (isogonal coaxial circles), a Möbius∘affine map (oblique coaxial circles, which is what Fig. 376 is as drawn) and the exact thin-plate spline, with the spread of the species about their own genus means as the noise floor and all 3782 ordered pairs of genus means as context. Thompson's own figure is cropped from the public-domain Internet Archive scan (page 749 as the original JPEG-2000 leaf, hash-pinned) and placed beside the data.

Among all genus pairs in the dataset, *Scarus* → *Pomacanthus* is at the 31st percentile for how much coaxial circles explain, so it is not even a favourable case; for *Scarus* → the 54 *Chaetodon* species the family explains a median 35 %. What remains is local — the anal-fin end, snout, dorsal-fin end and anal-fin origin carry the largest residuals — which is the kind of change Thompson's method was built to exclude. Non-tree encodings fall back to flat per-segment polygons.

### [models/1966-raup.py](./models/1966-raup.py)

Raup's model of shell coiling (Raup & Michelson 1965, *Science* 147:1294, doi:10.1126/science.147.3663.1294; Raup 1966, *J. Paleontol.* 40:1178–1190, doi:10.2307/1301992). A generating curve (the aperture) is swept around a fixed axis while every dimension grows as a logarithmic spiral: W is the whorl expansion rate (each whorl is the previous one magnified by W), D the inner/outer-margin ratio of the generating curve (its relative distance from the axis), T the axial movement of the curve's centre per unit of its radial movement (T = 0 is planispiral), S the curve's shape. `1966-raup.py` draws a 3 × 3 slice (W against T) of the morphospace; `create_raup_animations.py` renders six illustrative forms and the parameter-space sheets.

### [models/2001-bressloff-v1-form-constants.py](./models/2001-bressloff-v1-form-constants.py)

Bressloff, Cowan, Golubitsky, Thomas & Wiener's (2001/2002) Euclidean symmetry-breaking model of primary visual cortex (V1) and Klüver's four geometric hallucination form constants (tunnels/funnels, spirals, cobwebs, honeycombs), paired with Rule, Stoffregen & Ermentrout's (2011) flicker-phosphene Floquet resonance tongues (`1:1` vs `2:1` stripes vs hex/spots):
1. **Marginal stability and branch selection (Bressloff et al. 2001 §4–5, Figs. 16–17, 37–40):** Fourier–Bessel lateral coupling with the Mexican-hat surround, even/odd orientation eigenbranches $\lambda_\pm(q)$ and the critical wavenumber $q^*$ from the perturbative scan, cross-checked against a direct diagonalisation of the exact orientation-space operator ($|m| \le 8$: $|\Delta\lambda| = 3\times10^{-4}$, $\Delta q^* = 0$) whose dominant harmonic *derives* the contoured ($m = 1$) versus non-contoured ($m = 0$, Ermentrout–Cowan limit) regime — the switch sits at local inhibition $A_{loc} \approx 0.24$–$0.25$ where $W_0^{loc} = W_1^{loc}$, and fixture presets that impose a non-critical harmonic or parity are flagged. Pattern selection uses the **full** cubic amplitude equations (Eqs. 5.9–5.15): $\gamma(\theta) = [3 s_3 \Gamma_3 - 2 s_2^2 Q]/N$ with sigmoid firing-rate derivative weights at baseline $\sigma_0$ and the quadratic feedback $Q$ through the linear resolvent, $\eta$-aware $\Lambda_r < \Lambda_h$ thresholds, and roll stability tested against every perturbation direction $\theta$ (Lean: `bressloff_roll_stable_iff`, `bressloff_fig38_roll_unstable`); at $\sigma_0 = \tfrac12$ the coefficients reduce to the upstream bare overlaps ($\gamma(60°)/\gamma(0)$ 0.4864 vs 0.4870), which is kept as the parity path. All five `stability_reports` and all 24 preset `runs` in [`datasets/bressloff/paper-calibration.json`](./datasets/bressloff/paper-calibration.json) are reproduced to $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ — parity with the vendored MesmerPrism Rust implementation, not with digitised paper figures. The fixture's own paper-expectation checks are counted in full rather than hidden: 121/133 pass (`stability_reports` 10/11, `runs[].checks` 111/122), and every failure is listed in the CSV, the figure ledger and `--self-test` — the Fig. 40 odd-branch *triangle*→*honeycomb* failure is the $H_0/H_\pi$ degeneracy at cubic order ($\eta_{hex} = 0$); the `same-lattice-branch-*` failures on the square and hex presets are the same cubic-order limitation. ### [models/2013-chindapol-coral-flow.py](./models/2013-chindapol-coral-flow.py)

Chindapol et al.'s (2013) flow-driven growth of *Pocillopora verrucosa*. The paper couples polyp-based accretive growth to Navier–Stokes + advection–diffusion in 3D (COMSOL); this script makes a declared reduction to 2D diffusion-limited aggregation with drift, where the drift/diffusion ratio plays the role of the Péclet number, and recovers the paper's transition from symmetric branching (low Pe) to upstream-biased, compact colonies (high Pe). Outputs: `chindapol_coral_flow_morphospace.png` and `chindapol_coral_flow_morphospace_morphometrics.png` (branch-direction rose plots and surface/volume vs Pe); `--gif PATH` adds the growth replay below.

![Chindapol coral growth replayed polyp by polyp under no, moderate and strong flow](docs/media/chindapol_coral_flow_growth.gif)

*What you see:* three colonies growing polyp by polyp from one seed, viewed from above, under no flow, moderate flow and strong flow (flow runs left to right; colour = age, dark old to pale new). Each new polyp is placed where a nutrient random-walker first touches the colony, so growth follows nutrient flux; with flow the walkers arrive from upstream and the colony leans into the current and compacts, which is the paper's result.

Audit note (2026-10-13, 55.5): the reduction is explicit, and the paper's symmetry magnitude and branch alignment are computed, but they are not compared numerically with the paper's Figs 9–12.

**Reference:** Chindapol N, Kaandorp JA, Cronemberger C, Mass T, Genin A (2013). Modelling growth and form of the scleractinian coral *Pocillopora verrucosa* and the influence of hydrodynamics. *PLoS Comput Biol* 9(1):e1002849. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002849)

### [models/2010-petroff-stromatolite.py](./models/2010-petroff-stromatolite.py)

Petroff et al.'s (2010) biophysics of conical stromatolites: (1) the spacing law ℓ = α√(Dτ), which makes a ~1 cm spacing between neighbouring cones a fingerprint of a ~daylength photosynthetic rhythm and packs cones onto a hexagonal lattice, and (2) a dome-vs-cone morphospace from the deterministic KPZ growth law ∂h/∂t = v + λ(1 + ½(∂h/∂r)²), where β = v/λ sets the aspect ratio. Output: `petroff_stromatolite_morphospace.png`; `--gif PATH` adds the animation below.

![Petroff stromatolite: dome-to-cone sweep of beta = v/lambda](docs/media/petroff_stromatolite_dome_to_cone.gif)

*What you see:* a stromatolite cross-section grown lamina by lamina from a small seed under velocity = v·ẑ + λ·n̂ (vertical phototactic growth plus surface-normal mineral accretion), while the ratio β = v/λ sweeps 0.2 → 6 along the bar below: low β rounds the seed into a dome, high β extrudes a sharp cone. ### [models/2024-kahng-coral-geometry.py](./models/2024-kahng-coral-geometry.py)

Kahng, Odle & Wakeman's (2024) geometry of reef-coral colonies. A colony lays down skeleton at a per-area calcification rate G, so volume grows as V(t+1) = V(t) + G·S(t)/ρ; whether linear extension and surface-area productivity are size-dependent is then decided entirely by how the surface-to-volume ratio changes with size. The script reconstructs these relationships for the paper's four canonical shapes (hemispheroid, fixed-aspect cone, flat disc of fixed thickness, cylinder of fixed branch diameter). Output: `kahng_coral_geometry_morphospace.png`; `--gif PATH` adds the animation below.

![Kahng coral geometry: the master rule iterated year by year for four colony shapes](docs/media/kahng_coral_geometry_growth.gif)

*What you see:* the growth rule V(t+1) = V(t) + G·S/ρ iterated year by year with the same G and ρ for every shape, so only geometry differs. Left, the hemispheroid and the cone drawn to scale: their surface-to-volume ratio falls as they grow, so their extension plateaus and their surface-area productivity (SAP) drops. Right, surface area against time on a log axis: the flat disc and the fixed-diameter cylinder keep S:V constant and grow exponentially — the paper's size-independent SAP — while the two massive shapes level off.

**Reference:** Kahng SE, Odle E, Wakeman KC (2024). Coral geometry and why it matters. *PeerJ* 12:e17037. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.17037). Code: [ericodle/coral_geometry_modeling](https://github.com/ericodle/coral_geometry_modeling).

### [sorting-morphogenesis/](./sorting-morphogenesis/)

Zhang, Goldstein & Levin's (2025) model of autonomous cell-view sorting morphogenesis: 1D arrays of cells that sort themselves locally by Bubble, Insertion or Selection rules without a central controller, navigating around passive (`movable`) and stuck (`immovable`) frozen defects, exhibiting Delayed Gratification (temporary increases in monotonicity error followed by larger reductions), and spontaneously segregating by algotype in chimeric arrays. Includes deterministic seed-locked paired A/B lane comparisons (`--compare-ab`) and a 4-tier claim-boundary catalog ([`sorting-morphogenesis/catalog.json`](./sorting-morphogenesis/catalog.json)).

```bash
python3 sorting-morphogenesis/run_experiments.py --compare-ab --n 100   # seed-locked paired A/B comparison + CSV
python3 sorting-morphogenesis/run_experiments.py --gif zhang.gif --gif-only
```

![Zhang, Goldstein & Levin (2025): autonomous cell-view sorting around immovable frozen defects, Delayed Gratification, and chimeric algotype clustering](docs/media/zhang_sorting_morphogenesis.gif)

*What you see:* top-left, autonomous Selection vs Bubble cell arrays ($n = 36$) sorting around two immovable frozen defects (red outlines) — Selection jumps across the defects to near-zero error while Bubble is blocked locally; bottom-left, a 50/50 Bubble + Selection chimera with duplicate values $1..9$ where equal-valued cells spontaneously segregate by algotype (blue = Bubble, orange = Selection); right, the monotonicity-error trajectory with Delayed Gratification excursions and the algotype aggregation curve rising from the `0.50` random baseline to `~0.72`.

*Replication findings ($n = 100$, [`sorting-morphogenesis/README.md`](./sorting-morphogenesis/README.md), reported upstream in [Zhangtaining/cell_research#2](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research/issues/2)):* all quantitative claims in Sections 4.1–4.4 reproduce — Bubble and Insertion perform the same swap count as top-down ($\binom{n}{2}/2 \approx 2,482$) with **1.55×** and **2.04×** fewer total comparisons+swaps (paper: `1.50×`, `2.03×`); Selection performs **11.05×** more swaps (`1,046.9` vs `94.7`, paper: `~11×`) when `MultiThreadCell.swap()` resets `ideal_position` to `0` on each swap, and **1.18×** more total operations (paper: `1.17×`) when `ideal_position` persists; Selection achieves lower final error on immovable frozen defects (`1.00, 1.95, 2.95` vs `1.80, 3.75, 5.10` for $f=1..3$, Fig. 5C) because its `ideal_position` pointer swaps non-locally across barriers; Insertion avoids halting behind a frozen defect via an explicit prefix-reset rule (`InsertionSortCell.is_enable_to_move` L68–82); Delayed Gratification in Bubble rises monotonically with $f=0..3$ (`0.38 → 0.51` vs traditional `< 0.23`, Fig. 7A); chimeric arrays (`B+S`, `I+S`, `B+I`) peak at `0.635–0.640` algotype aggregation on unique values (Fig. 8A) and stay permanently segregated (`0.698` and `0.661`, Fig. 8D) on duplicate values `1..10`; and opposite-direction chimeras follow the paper's `Bubble > Selection > Insertion` sortedness hierarchy (`B+I = 75.6%`, `B+S = 47.3%`, `I+S = 40.6%`, Figs. 9–10).

**Reference:** Zhang T, Goldstein A, Levin M (2025). Classical sorting algorithms as a model of morphogenesis: Self-sorting arrays reveal unexpected competencies in a minimal model of basal intelligence. *Adaptive Behavior* 33(1):25–54. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/10597123241269740). Code: [Zhangtaining/cell_research](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research) (pinned commit `1fd2bd59`; replication notes in [issue #2](https://github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research/issues/2)).

### Extending the repository

A new reproduction is one script, one dataset folder, one audit. The 12 steps the loop tracks are the checklist; the first six are what you do, the last six are what MorphoEval does to it.

1. **Scout and read the paper.** Pick a published model of form, a formal database of forms, or a measurable claim. Decide which of the three kinds the script will be — reproduce the model from its equations, decode the authors' encoding, or test the claim — and say so in the docstring.
2. **Vendor or pin the formal source.** Create `datasets/<name>/` with a `README.md` (source, licence, retrieval date, what was changed), `paper_citations.bib`, and either the files themselves (small, licence permitting) or their SHA-256 in a manifest with a `--download` path into a git-ignored `.<name>_cache/` (add it to `.gitignore`). Record the hash of anything derived from the raw source (as `datasets/conus/conus_textile_reference.json` and `datasets/planform/planform_bioelectric_series_v1.json` do). Read the authors' code where it exists to settle what they meant; do not copy it.
3. **Pin the rules.** Turn the paper's prose into exact rules and verify them on every record before drawing anything (the Planform and Limbform decoders check their radius rule on all 1181 regions / 3146 joints at load time; the Raup script recovers W, D, T from its own mesh and refuses to draw if they differ).
4. **Write `<year>-<author>-<model>.py`.** Self-contained, run with `python3`, outputs (PNG/GIF/CSV) written to the working directory and git-ignored. Give it `--self-test` (deterministic checks, input hashes, fast), `--gif PATH`, a `--no-show`/offline path, and seeded RNGs. Put a real specimen or the authors' figure beside the output and measure the distance between them; distinct forms must come out distinct.
5. **Make the media.** Downsize the script's GIF into `docs/media/` as described in [`docs/media/README.md`](./docs/media/README.md) and add a row there; re-run `docs/media/make_montage.py` and `morphoeval/report/draw_pipeline.py --gif` after adding the tile.
6. **Document.** Add the script to the index table and a section in this README (what it does, commands, "What you see", references), following the existing ones.
7. **Audit.** Copy the nearest `morphoeval/audits/<date>-<name>.yaml`, score the seven dimensions with line-pinned evidence at a recorded commit, and ideally have a second model from another company score it blind. `python3 morphoeval/build_morphoeval_kg.py <audit> --check` validates it; `measure_outputs.py` fingerprints and measures the outputs; `build_morphoeval_kg.py --conform --query` builds the nanopubs; `sign_morphoeval.py` signs (test registry first, then `--publish prod`); `check_signed.mjs` verifies independently. Commit `morphoeval/kg/<audit>/` — it is the checkpoint. The full workflow, with the re-score and `--supersede-from` conventions, is in [`morphoeval/README.md`](./morphoeval/README.md#workflow).
8. **State the mathematics.** Add a section to [`morphoeval/lean/MathOfBiology.lean`](./morphoeval/lean/MathOfBiology.lean) with a definition and at least one theorem per governing law (core Lean, no `sorry`; `lean morphoeval/lean/MathOfBiology.lean` must exit 0), and a matching entry in `MATH_LAWS_REGISTRY` in `morphoeval/math_kg.py` (formula, Lean declaration and theorem names, Lean and Python line loci, source equation). `python3 morphoeval/math_kg.py --build` then puts the law, its proof status and the audit's measurements into the graph; `--sim <id>` shows the result.
9. **Track it** (optional). `morphoeval/track_loop.py init --run-id <id> --target <sim-id>` then `start`/`end` or `exec` around each step; `summary --md` writes the per-step table (see [`morphoeval/runs/2026-10-18-runions-venation-loop.md`](./morphoeval/runs/2026-10-18-runions-venation-loop.md) for a complete worked sample run).

Re-scoring an existing script after a fix follows step 7 with a new audit file that names the one it `supersedes`; published nanopubs are never edited.

#### [Nanopublications](./nanopubs/)

Nanopublication-related files including wave files (.trig format), hypothesis mappings, and scripts for processing and canonicalizing nanopublications. These tools enable structured representation and sharing of scientific findings in a machine-readable format. The MorphoEval nanopubs use their own signer and verifier (see [`morphoeval/README.md`](./morphoeval/README.md)) because nanopub-py 2.1 does not work on Python 3.14.

**See [`nanopubs/README.md`](./nanopubs/README.md) for details.**
