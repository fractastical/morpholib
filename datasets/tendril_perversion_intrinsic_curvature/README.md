## Governing Mathematical & Biomechanical Equations

### 1. Kirchhoff Elastic Rod Potential Energy with Intrinsic Curvature $\kappa_0$
For an inextensible Kirchhoff rod of length $L$, bending stiffness $B = E I$, twisting stiffness $C = \mu J$, twist-to-bend stiffness ratio $\Gamma = C / B = 1 / (1 + \nu)$, intrinsic material curvature $\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(0)} = (\kappa_0, 0, 0)$ generated by differential contraction $\Delta\varepsilon_g$ of the lignified gelatinous G-fiber ribbon (`Gerbode et al. 2012`), and axial tension $T$:
$$E[\theta, \phi, \psi] = \int_{-L/2}^{L/2} \left[ \frac{B}{2}\left((\kappa_1 - \kappa_0)^2 + \kappa_2^2\right) + \frac{C}{2}(\kappa_3 - \tau_0)^2 - T \cos\theta \right] ds.$$

### 2. Zero-Link Conservation ($\Delta\text{Lk} = 0$) & Chiral Helix Hand-Reversal
When both ends of a climbing tendril are tethered to supports and cannot rotate freely, Călugăreanu–White–Fuller topological link conservation enforces zero net linking number change ($\Delta\text{Lk} = \text{Tw} + \text{Wr} = 0$). Consequently, coiling proceeds via a pair of mirror-symmetric helices of opposite chirality ($+\tau$ right-handed and $-\tau$ left-handed, with equal winding numbers $N_+ = N_-$) separated by a flat **tendril perversion** inversion kink where Frenet torsion $\tau(0) = 0$ and curvature reaches a local extremum.

### 3. Critical Tension Pitchfork Bifurcation & Heteroclinic Perversion Soliton
Under decreasing axial tension $T$ (or increasing intrinsic curvature $\kappa_0$ during G-fiber lignification and dehydration), the straight rod ($\theta = 0$) loses stability at the critical pitchfork bifurcation tension (`Goriely & Tabor 1998` Eq. 11; `McMillen & Goriely 2002` Sec. 3):
$$T_c = \frac{B \kappa_0^2}{1 + \Gamma}, \qquad \cos\theta_{\text{helix}}(T) = 1 - \frac{1 + \Gamma}{\Gamma}\left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{T}{T_c}}\right) \quad (T \le T_c).$$
Across the perversion inversion center $s = 0$, the local inclination angle connects the two opposite-handed helical states via the heteroclinic kink profile:
$$\theta(s) = \theta_{\text{helix}} \tanh(s / \xi), \qquad \tau(s) = \tau_{\text{helix}} \tanh(s / \xi), \qquad \xi = \sqrt{\frac{B}{T_c - T}}.$$

### 4. Soft vs Old Lignified Tendril Over-Winding (`Gerbode et al. 2012` Fig. 3–4)
In young soft tendrils (or asymmetric PDMS composite strips with $\Gamma = C/B < 1$), initial axial pulling *overwinds* the tendril ($\Delta N > 0$, increasing coil turns before unwinding at high tension) because twist energy is cheaper than bending energy ($\Gamma < 1$). In mature lignified tendrils where ectoplasmic lignification elevates torsional rigidity ($\Gamma > 1$), axial stretching monotonically unwinds the coil ($\Delta N < 0$).
