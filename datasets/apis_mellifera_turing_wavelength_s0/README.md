# *Apis mellifera* — Turing wavelength at 11 µm (detail), Holocene 0 Ma

Slot 3115 of the 10,000-animal basic look. One animal, one scale, one age.

- **Animal:** *Apis mellifera* (Western honeybee). Aristotelian place: II. Anaima (Invertebrata) → Entoma (Arthropoda) → Apis.
- **Scale:** 11 µm detail variant of tier `4_cellular_10um`. λ_c = 1.12×10⁻⁵ m = 11.2 µm, log10(λ_c) = −4.951. This is the selected Turing wavenumber of an epidermal activator–inhibitor field, not the millimetre-scale comb.
- **Age:** Holocene (extant), 0 Ma. Apis chronoseries 1/1. The series has no sampled predecessor or successor chronospecies. The developmental predecessor of the patterned state is the sub-threshold epidermis (equal diffusivities, σ(k_c) < 0).

The specimen photograph is a public-domain Wikimedia image of a worker extracting nectar (John Severns, File:`European_honey_bee_extracts_nectar.jpg`). The species-gallery JPEG is a synthetic amber field and is not used as the fitness target.

Primary quantitative law: Turing wavenumber k_c = argmax σ(k), with λ_c = 2π / k_c. Kinetics are Schnakenberg (1979), the dispersion relation is Turing (1952), and the nearest published cellular length is the ocellar rhabdom sheet of Ribi, Warrant & Zeil (2011), 8.7 µm × 1 µm. That length is reported beside 11.2 µm; the model is not retuned to pretend they are equal.

Sibling slot `apis-mellifera-turing-wavelength` (Camazine 1991) is comb self-organization at about 5.2 mm. Its parameters are not reused. k_c here is roughly 470 times the comb-cell wavenumber.
