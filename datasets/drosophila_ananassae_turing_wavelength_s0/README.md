# *Drosophila ananassae* — Turing wavelength, detail scale (slot 3176)

Holocene (extant) pantropical pineapple fly, lineage Sophophora ananassae–montium. This slot is Drosophila chronoseries 5/41 at geological age **0 Ma**.

**Scale:** 2.1 µm (2.106 × 10⁻⁶ m, log10 = −5.677, tier `3_subcellular_um`). The quantity selected by the Turing wavenumber k_c is an intracellular pigment / ocellus wavelength, not the 85 µm cellular stripe of sibling slot 2841 and not the chronospecies wavelengths of the predecessor *Drosophila americana* (139 µm) or the successor *Drosophila biarmipes* (96 µm).

## What changes at this scale

At fixed Schnakenberg kinetics, wavelength scales as the square root of diffusivity. Collapsing from the americana primordium wavelength (139 µm) to 2.106 µm shortens the diffusion length by a factor of about 66 and the diffusivity by about 4.3 × 10³. That is the intracellular, vesicle-corralled regime (ommochrome-precursor activator, faster cytosolic inhibitor) inside one retinal or epidermal cell. The biarmipes successor re-expands the wavelength to 96 µm when a wing-spot enhancer deploys pigment across many cells (Gompel et al. 2005, 10.1038/nature03235). *D. ananassae* is diagnosed by the transverse sex comb and by optomotor-blind chiasma variants; this script does **not** claim that those organs are generated by the 2.1 µm mode.

`drosophila_ananassae_specimen.png` is the public-domain Wikimedia Commons plate `File:Drosophila ananassae female.png` (225 × 112). It is a species illustration, not a new micrograph at 2.1 µm. The simulation paints the Schnakenberg field as a cuticular pigment modulation on that plate. The synthetic spiral in `datasets/species_gallery/` was not used.

## Model

Schnakenberg (1979) kinetics on a periodic grid. Nominal parameters are local to this slot: a = 0.11, b = 1.15, D_v/D_u = 19.7. Physical pixel size is set so the fastest-growing linear mode has wavelength 2.106 µm. Turing (1952) supplies the dispersion relation; Gierer & Meinhardt (1972) and Kondo & Miura (2010) supply the activator–inhibitor reading. The *D. ananassae* genome in the 12 Genomes Consortium (2007) paper places the species; Ready, Hanson & Benzer (1976) place the retinal lattice at a few micrometres; Wittkopp, Carroll & Kopp (2003) review pigment-pattern genetics.
