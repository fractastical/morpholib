# Luigi Galvani (1791–1794) & Subsequent Electrophysiology — *De Viribus Electricitatis* & Quantitative Current-to-Twitch Atlas

This directory contains SHA-256-verified archival scans of all four original fold-out copperplate engravings (`Tab. I` through `Tab. IV`, engraved by Jacopo Zambelli) plus the 1791 Bologna title page (`Ex Typographia Instituti Scientiarum`) and *Pars Prima* opening folio (p. 363) from Luigi Galvani's foundational treatise on bioelectricity, together with five subsequent historical electrophysiology plates:
- Wellcome Collection isolated sciatic-nerve dissection (`M0012614`, `Tav. I`, `Figs. 1–4` from the 1792 Modena / 1794 Italian treatise),
- **The Galvanoscopic Frog** (`L0011450`, `Fig. 8` — Carlo Matteucci's 1838–1842 microampere biological current detector),
- **Giovanni Aldini's (1794/1803) Metal-Free Nerve-Muscle Loop** (`L0001964`, `Fig. 3`),
- **Carlo Matteucci's (1842–1845) Series Frog Battery (`Pile de Grenouilles`) & Induced Secondary Contraction** (`Matteucci's frog battery trimmed2.jpg`), and
- **Dr. Andrew Ure's (1818) High-Current 270-Plate Voltaic Trough Demonstration on Matthew Clydesdale** (`Figuier Fig. 333`).

## Hindlimb kinematic model

Wellcome `L0002011` Fig. 3 is cropped and warped with a **4-joint rigid chain** landmarked on the copperplate and named from Ecker & Gaupp, *Anatomie des Frosches* (1896–1904):

| Segment | Joints | Muscle overlay |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Urostyle + ilium | atlas → sacrum → hip | — |
| Femur (thigh) | hip → knee | triceps femoris (preaxial), semimembranosus / gracilis (postaxial) |
| Tibiofibula (shank) | knee → ankle | tibialis anticus (preaxial) |
| Astragalus–calcaneum + digits | ankle → tarsus → toe | **gastrocnemius / plantaris longus** (biarticular: knee + ankle) |

Projected Fig. 3 `TL/FL ≈ 1.15` (Gaupp 3-D target `1.05–1.15`). Full-twitch foot sweep is `65°`, matching the Fe/Cu arc kick in `current_to_twitch_experiments`. Landmarks and angles live in `galvani_1791_reference.json` → `hindlimb_anatomy`. Lean 4 Law 4 in `Loop_101` proves length conservation, the TL/FL band, and the biarticular gastrocnemius identity.
